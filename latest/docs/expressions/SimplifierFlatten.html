All-purpose Utilities

Flatten rule of ExpressionSimplifier (PR B)

Status: first n-ary treatment of the simplifier, restricted to the linear additive chains; §11 describes the shared metadata of mission C3a, §12 the term analysis and the spine chains of mission C3b, §13 the global factoring of mission C3c, §14 the stabilization of mission C3d, §15 the trigonometric rule and the linear quotient chains, §16 the grouped quotient chains. Built on the infrastructure of FlattenArchitecture.md (PR A, a9ec71ac). No multiplicative chain, no division, no C3, no change to ExpressionDerivation<T> or ExpressionIntegration<T>.

1. Purpose

Level by level, a sum of n terms costs n - 1 node treatments, and each of them prepares its operands, dispatches the additive rules, runs the factor comparisons of AdditionOfEqualsElements / SubstractionOfEqualsElements (each comparison simplifies both operands again through the comparer) and canonicalizes the whole sub-chain. On a left-associated chain the sub-chain grows by one term per level, and the comparer simplifies a canonical chain that is not a fixed point of the subtraction rules (S5A-12): x0 - x1 - ... - x15 with free parameters is refused by the default budget.

The flatten rule treats a whole chain at once: the terms are prepared once, and the canonicalization runs once.

2. B0 audit: the level by level pipeline

For a binary node e, TransformCore(e) does, in this order:

  1. PrepareExpression(left), then PrepareExpression(right): for the simplifier, FoldNaNOperand(TransformCore(child)). The children are therefore complete results, canonicalized and NaN-folded, before the node sees them.
  2. The node is rebuilt from the prepared children (CopyBinaryExpression).
  3. The candidate rules of the node type run in plan order; the effect gate (AllowsRules) is asked once, when the first candidate matches. The first rule that returns a result ends the treatment: no finalization, no canonicalization.
  4. Otherwise FinalizeExpression: for an ordinary Add/Subtract of a type whose operator cannot throw, CanonicalizeAdditiveExpression(left, right), which collects the terms of both prepared operands (CollectAdditiveTerms, through ordinary Add, Subtract and Negate), pins the terms that are not inert, drops zero constants, aggregates the constants when the level holds at least two of them, sorts (stable), groups, cancels and rebuilds a right-associated chain.
  5. The result goes back to the parent, whose PrepareExpression applies FoldNaNOperand to it.

Consequences that matter for an n-ary treatment:

  • The result of an inner node is what its parent's rules match. A canonical sub-chain is an Add node (or 0 - t, a Subtract); a single term, a constant or a product can also come up when terms cancel.
  • A rule result is not canonical. SubstractionWithAddition returns (a - b) - c as built; the factoring rules return (a + b) * k; AdditionWithZero(t, 0) transforms t again.
  • The sort is stable and free parameters tie (S4: two free parameters of one type have no order), so the order of tied terms is the order in which the level collected them, which depends on the grouping and on the rules that ran below.
  • Constants are aggregated per level, only when a level holds two of them.

Hence the level by level result of a chain is not a function of its signed terms: a + a is 2 * a, a + (b + a) is (a + a) + b; 2*a + 2*b is (a + b) * 2; a - (b + c) is (a - b) - c; (c - b) - a is (0 - b) + ((0 - a) + c) and simplifying that again gives (0 - a) + ((0 - b) + c); ((a + 5) - 5) - 3 is (0 - 3) + a while (a + 5) - 8 is -3 + a.

3. Linear chains

A chain rooted at an ordinary Add or Subtract node is linear when (ExpressionSimplifier.IsLinearChain):

  1. its type is double, float, int, long, uint or ulong, and every crossed node is an ordinary operator (IsOrdinaryBinaryArithmetic); the crossed nodes are the Add and Subtract nodes the default CanFlattenThrough accepts (no operator method, intrinsic non-nullable result type), so decimal, checked, lifted and user-defined operators are operands, and a Negate is an operand too (it is not declared);
  2. no Subtract has a crossed right operand, and no crossed node has two constant operands;
  3. every term is a linear term: a parameter; a finite non-zero constant (a boxed primitive of exactly the chain type); or an ordinary product of a parameter by another parameter, or by a finite constant other than 0, 1 and -1;
  4. no parameter occurs twice in the chain (as a term or as a factor), no constant coefficient value occurs twice, and no coefficient equals a constant term;
  5. the chain holds at most two constant terms.

3.1 Preparing a linear term

A parameter and a constant are their own preparation. A product c * x, x * c or x * y is prepared by the multiplicative rules into a product (Multiplication(left, const) commutes the constant first; the zero and one identities do not match; MultiplicationOfEqualsElements compares two distinct operands and declines; the finalization orders the factors). No user code runs, nothing throws, and the prepared term is never a sum, a difference, a negation or a constant. The NaN fold cannot apply (no non-finite constant).

3.2 No rule applies at any level

By induction on the levels, with the matrix above. The only rule that runs is the factoring rule, and its comparisons fail:

  • two operands of one node never hold the same parameter (condition 4), and the comparer's simplifier never introduces a parameter, so L' and R', or a factor of one and the other operand, cannot simplify to equal trees;
  • a constant coefficient is compared with another coefficient (distinct values), with a parameter, or with a constant term (distinct from every coefficient);
  • the constant 1 the rule supplies for an operand that is not a product is compared with a factor, never 1; an operand that is the constant 1 makes the rule decline before any comparison.

Nothing cancels (no repeated parameter), so a sub-chain with k non-constant terms keeps k groups, and every group holds one term.

3.3 The per-level canonicalizations compose

Every level therefore ends with CanonicalizeAdditiveExpression on its two operands. Its result is rebuilt from one-term groups in sorted order (t or 0 - t), and the parent collects it back in that same order. A stable sort of the concatenation of two stably sorted lists is the stable sort of the concatenation of their sources, so by induction the root orders the terms as one stable sort of the source order does. The key comparer is the same at every level (one lexical scope: a chain never crosses a binder).

Constants: with at most two constant terms, the level where the second one arrives is the only aggregation, with the two constants in source order, exactly as one aggregation of the whole list does. With three or more it differs (found by the differential campaign): a floating-point sum depends on the grouping, and when the first two cancel the third stays alone and is not rewritten (0 - 3 instead of -3).

So the level by level result is CanonicalizeAdditiveTerms(prepared terms in source order), which is what the rule returns. The helper is the body of CanonicalizeAdditiveExpression, extracted without change; both paths share it.

3.4 What is refused, and what is still accepted below it

Everything else is refused before anything is prepared: a repeated parameter (a + a, a*b + a, a shared sub-chain), equal coefficients (2*a + 2*b), a coefficient equal to a constant term (2 + 2*a), a zero, NaN or infinite constant, the coefficients 0, 1, -1, a subtracted group (a - (b + c)), a pair of constants (a + (1 + 2)), a third constant, a negation, a call, a power, a division, a conversion, a nested product, any node with effects, short, ushort and decimal chains.

A refused chain is transformed from its root exactly as before, and the chains below it are offered in turn: in (x0 - x1 - ... - x9) + Sin(y) the root is refused and its left operand is a linear chain of its own. This also applies to the additive nodes the rules rebuild and transform again (a - (-b) → a + b), and to the trees the comparer simplifies.

Limit: the decision is all or nothing per chain root. One term that is not linear, or one repetition, anywhere in the chain sends the root to the level by level treatment, with its historical cost; only the linear sub-chains below the refused node are treated at once. On a left-associated chain whose last term is the offending one, the whole prefix is a linear sub-chain and most of the gain remains; when the offending term comes first, or when the repetition is far from the start, the levels above the repetition pay the historical cost. Example: the S5 family p (100*x0 - x1*3 - 4*x2 - ..., coefficient i + 2) repeats its first coefficient at term 98: up to 97 terms it is accepted (linear cost), from 99 terms the levels above term 98 are treated as on master, whose cost on this family grows too fast for 256 terms to finish in hours (measured: stopped after three hours on the branch; the same tree is not expected to be faster on master). Splitting a refused chain into maximal linear runs is the natural next step and is not part of this PR: it changes which terms are canonicalized together, so it needs its own proof of compatibility.

4. Mechanism

  • [Flatten(ExpressionType.Add, ExpressionType.Subtract)] private Expression? FlattenAdditiveChain(Expression source, FlattenedTerm[] terms, FlattenRuleDispatcher dispatcher): prepares each term once, in source order, with PrepareExpression (transformation, then NaN fold), collects it with CollectAdditiveTerms, and returns CanonicalizeAdditiveTerms. It never declines. Since the dispatcher mission it receives the dispatcher of its group, the additive signature rules of the plan, which the anchored fallback uses (§10.3, FlattenArchitecture.md §21.6).
  • FlattenAdmission(root) (new engine-internal hook, see FlattenArchitecture.md §5): the built-in simplifier admits the linear chains only. The test is one iterative walk of the crossed nodes that stops at the first refusal, reads framework operators and leaves by type tests only (an extension node is never read, an operator method is only tested for provenance), counts one structural visit per node, and is linear in the distinct nodes of a shared DAG.
  • The effect gate is not asked for the built-in type: a linear chain only holds parameters, primitive constants and ordinary products under operators that cannot throw, a subset of what ExpressionEffectAnalysis.HasInertOperands proves inert (a test asserts it on every accepted chain of the differential corpus). The gate analyzes the operands recursively, which would bound the depth of an accepted chain by the execution stack.
  • A chain that is not admitted reports RuleRejected with the new reason ExpressionRuleRejection.ChainNotAdmitted (detailed trace) and is not extracted.

4.1 Derived simplifiers

The rule is private. BuildPlan scans GetMethods(Public | NonPublic | Instance) of the concrete type, which does not return the private methods of a base type: the plan of a derived simplifier holds no flatten rule, and its PrepareExpression, FinalizeExpression, own rules and hiding rules see every node as before. A derived simplifier that declares its own [Flatten] rule opts in for itself, and keeps the effect gate as its admission. No public extension point was added. The comparisons a derived simplifier triggers still simplify with the built-in type (as before), which now flattens the linear chains it meets; results are unchanged.

4.2 Policies

The operands of a linear chain are inert: the gate admits every rule on every node of it under every ExpressionSimplifierOptions, no audited rule runs outside the gate, and the NaN fold of a non-default policy keeps the historical decision on inert arithmetic. The rule is therefore policy-independent; AuditedRules, CanDiscard, CanRepeat and CanReorder are untouched.

4.3 The prepared-operand entry

TransformCoreWithPreparedOperands (two calls, both in AdditionWithNegate) still does not go through the flatten path: it runs the rules and the finalization of one rebuilt Subtract node on operands that are already prepared. It is only reached from a node with a Negate operand, which is never part of a linear chain, so no accepted chain depends on it, and its results are those of master. It was left unchanged on purpose: routing it to the flatten path would hand prepared operands to a rule that prepares its terms.

A rule that calls TransformCore on a rebuilt additive node (SubstractionWithNegate, NegateWithSubstraction, AdditionWithZero, the difference of AdditionWithNegate when no form is reused) offers that node as a chain: its operands are prepared results, so it is linear only if they are linear terms or linear sub-chains, and then §3 applies to it as to a source chain (preparing a prepared linear term again is what the level by level path does too).

  • MaxDepth and MaxNodes are validated before any rule, as before: a chain deeper than 512 nodes is refused by the default limits although the rule would treat it without recursion.

6. Compatibility

Under every policy, for the built-in type: same result trees (structure, types, constants, parameter instances) for the first and the second pass, same user-code calls in the same order, same exceptions, as master a9ec71ac, except the resource refusals that became acceptances (§5). Derived simplifiers: unchanged, counters included, except for the work of the comparisons they trigger. Traces of the built-in type differ on the chains concerned (one treatment per chain, the flatten rule as the applied rule, ChainNotAdmitted rejections at the detailed level).

9. Refused chains: known refusals (mission C1)

Status: optimization of the admission only. No admission decision changes, so no result, user-code call or exception changes; only the work spent on refused chains decreases.

9.1 C0 characterization

A refused root is transformed level by level (§3.4), and every crossed node below it is offered in turn to the admission as the root of a chain of its own. Each offer walked its chain from its own root, so when the cause of the refusal lies deep (a call at the bottom of a left chain, a repetition between the last term and a deep one), each level walked down to the cause again: n²/2 node visits, and the operand sets rebuilt as many times. 9.2 Strategies studied

  • A. Propagating proven refusals (retained). Every cause of a refusal is inherited by every chain that contains it: a local cause belongs to a crossed node that a larger chain crosses the same way (an intrinsic operator has the type of its operands); a global cause involves terms that a larger chain holds too, and the sets only grow. The converse is false: an admitted chain says nothing about a larger one (a + b and c + a are admitted, (a + b) + (c + a) is not), so nothing is inferred from an admission.
  • B. Bottom-up summaries. Composing per-node summaries (types, parameters, coefficients, constants) would decide every sub-chain from one pass, but the offers come top-down (the root first), the summaries either copy their sets (quadratic memory) or share them (a mutable structure behind a recorded decision), and the admitted case would pay for summaries it never reads. Not retained.
  • C. An operation-local cache of decisions. Caching every decision by node is not needed: an admitted root is extracted and its nodes are never offered again (except through a shared DAG), so only refusals are re-asked; A is that cache restricted to proven refusals, filled by the walk that proves them.

9.3 Mechanism

  • The walk (IsLinearChain) is unchanged in its decisions and order. It now reports where the cause lies on the spine: the path it follows from the root while every node has a single crossed operand, up to the first node with two (whose subtree holds everything visited afterwards). A spine node at index i contains every operand met at an index at least i; the walk reports the index of the refusing node (local cause), of the first constant (third constant), or the refusing node and the repeated key (repetition).
  • On a refusal, RecordRefusedSpine follows the spine again and records, in the table of the operation (ExpressionResourceBudget.Session.FlattenRefusals, an ExpressionNodeSet), the spine nodes from index 1 down to the smallest index involved: for a repetition, down to the first node that holds an earlier occurrence of the key (each node is recorded before its own terms are tested). The root itself is not recorded (it is being decided), nor anything below the first node with two crossed operands.
  • An offered root found in the table is refused by one lookup, counted as one structural visit.
  • The operand sets keep their first four entries inline (SmallOperandSet), so a chain refused within its first few terms allocates nothing (the H family).

Invariants.

  • Only proven refusals are recorded: every recorded node contains the cause. Decisions are a function of the immutable node alone (framework operator nodes, runtime types by reference, boxed primitives, parameter references), so an entry is valid in any context of the operation: another instance, a nested operation (a comparison), a DAG occurrence.
  • Lifetime: the outermost operation of the thread. The outermost Exit drops the table (in finally, so after an exception too); a nested operation shares it; another thread has its own session. No global state.
  • The table is filled and read by the exact built-in type only (a derived simplifier keeps the effect gate).
  • No user code: the recording pass reads crossed framework nodes and the linear terms the walk accepted, by type tests; keys are compared as the sets compare them (reference for a parameter, value for a boxed primitive). An entry is a node reference in an open-addressing table, so no Equals/GetHashCode of a node ever runs.
  • Budgets: the walk counts one structural visit per node as before, the recording pass one per recorded node, each lookup one. A budget refusal in the middle of the recording pass leaves a correct table (every entry already proven).

10. Mixed chains: anchored chains (mission C2)

Status: first treatment of chains that are not linear. Restricted to one family whose level by level result is proven to be one canonicalization; every other mixed chain keeps the level by level treatment (with the known refusals of §9).

10.1 C2 audit: which segments can be treated at once

A refused chain is processed level by level: each level prepares its two operands (the lower level's result and a term), then runs its rules and its finalization on the prepared node. A treatment of several levels at once must reproduce what those rules would have done with the operands they would have seen. The categories of segments:

  1. A sub-tree that is itself an admitted chain (a linear sub-chain below a refused node): already treated at once by PR B when the fallback offers it (§3.4). Nothing new to gain.
  2. A path of nodes of the spine (the levels from the root down to the node that holds a non-linear term): the candidates of this mission. Their result depends on the rules that run at each level.
  3. A run of terms that is not a sub-tree (x2 + x3 inside ((x1 + x2) + x3) + F()): never a unit of the level by level treatment; regrouping it would reassociate the source. Not treated.
  4. A segment separated by an operation with effects: the effect gate refuses every rule of a node whose prepared operands are not inert; this is what makes category 2 tractable (below).
  5. A segment where a factorization may apply (2*x + 2*y, x - x, a + (-a)): the factoring and negation rules change the structure the parent sees; such a segment keeps the level by level treatment.
  6. A segment whose canonicalization depends on its neighbours (pinned terms reading mutable state, constants summed per level, ties of the stable sort): composable only under the conditions of §10.2.

Approach A, maximal admitted sub-trees: already obtained by PR B (category 1); nothing new. Approach C, incremental summaries of the canonical result along the path: it would have to reproduce the rules of every level, including the comparisons of the factoring rules, whose user code (the simplifications of the comparer, custom Equals) runs once per level and per comparison; a summary that skips them changes the user code, one that runs them is the level by level treatment. Not retained. Approach B, segments of a path (retained) for the segments on which no rule can run at any level: those where the effect gate refuses every rule.

10.2 Anchored chains

A chain rooted at an ordinary Add or Subtract node is anchored when it satisfies every condition of a linear chain (§3) except that exactly one term, the anchor, is not linear, and:

  1. the anchor is a static call of a runtime method (ExpressionReflectionProvenance.IsRuntime) whose execution is not inert for the effect analysis (ExpressionEffectAnalysis.MethodEffects: an unknown method, which is observable, or a function of the allow-list that may throw, such as double.Clamp); Math.Sin and the other total functions are inert and are not anchors (their rules may apply level by level);
  2. the anchor is held by a node of the spine: every node above it has a single crossed operand (F() + x1 + ... + xN in a left grouping, x0 + (... + (xN + F())) in a right one, any comb of the two); the other operand of the anchor's node may be a crossed linear chain (x0 + ... + x50 + F() + x51 + ... + xN), prepared as a unit;
  3. the instance uses the historical admission (ExpressionSimplifierOptions.Default): under an explicit policy the audited rules may run outside the gate, so the chain keeps the level by level treatment there.

The repetition conditions of §3 apply to the other terms (the anchor is left out of the sets). The test is the walk of §9, which accepts the anchor when it meets it on the spine; its refusals keep the inheritance of §9.2 (a second anchor refuses the chains that hold both, an anchor below a branching node every chain above it), so the known refusals stay correct.

10.3 Why one canonicalization is the level by level result

Let the segment be the nodes from the root down to the anchor's node, and its operands the terms of those nodes and the other operand of the anchor's node, in source order.

  1. No rule runs at any level. Every node of the segment contains the anchor. Its prepared operands hold the prepared anchor (the anchor's node directly, every node above it through the canonical result of the level below, which keeps every pinned term). When the prepared anchor is not inert, AllowsRules (HasInertOperands) refuses the node, and the historical admission admits no audited rule outside the gate (AllowsAuditedRulesOutsideGate is false): every level is its finalization, CanonicalizeAdditiveExpression of its two prepared operands (the nodes are ordinary, so CanCanonicalizeCommutativeBinary and CanonicalizesOperator hold).
  2. The canonicalizations compose. In CanonicalizeAdditiveTerms, the anchor is pinned (not inert); when it is observable, every term reading mutable state is pinned too (parameters, products), the constants being the only kept terms; when it only may throw, the kept terms are the linear ones. Pinned terms keep their collection order and are appended after the kept groups, so each level collects the pinned terms of the level below in source order, followed by its own term: the pinned list of the root is the source order. The kept terms are distinct linear terms (no repetition, coefficient distinct from the constants), so, as on a linear chain (§3.3), every group holds one term, nothing cancels, the stable sorts compose, and at most two constants are aggregated once, in source order. A 0 - t group is collected back as a zero (dropped) and t with its sign.
  3. The fold of NaN changes nothing: no level has a NaN constant operand (the constants are finite).

Hence the result is CanonicalizeAdditiveTerms of the collected prepared operands of the segment, in source order, which FlattenAnchoredChain returns.

Preparation, order, user code. The operands are prepared once each with PrepareExpression, in source order; the anchor is prepared as a call, its arguments with it, exactly as its node prepares it. The levels only interleave their canonicalizations, which run no user code here: the anchor is pinned, so it is never keyed, grouped or compared; the effect analysis reads it by type tests and runtime reflection; the rebuilt nodes read its type, the return type of a runtime method. Preparing a linear term runs no user code either (§3.1). So the observable order of user code and of exceptions is the level by level one.

Fallback. The proof needs the prepared anchor to be a call of a runtime method that is not inert. The admission only knows the raw call; if a rule rewrote it (none of the current rules does for an anchor: the probe of the audit found no case), the rule runs the levels one by one on the prepared operands, from the anchor's node up, then the NaN fold of each child result, as its parent's PrepareExpression does: the historical treatment, without preparing anything twice. Each level is the node rebuilt from its prepared operands, the additive signature rules through the group's FlattenRuleDispatcher, then FinalizeExpression when none applies (until the dispatcher mission: TransformCoreWithPreparedOperands, which runs the same candidates, admission and finalization; results and budgets are unchanged, a trace shows a dispatch treatment per level, without its Finalize event). The audit counters can force this path (FlattenAdmissionStatistics.ForceAnchoredFallback); the tests compare it with the level by level treatment on every anchored case.

10.4 What is not treated

  • A chain whose non-linear term is inert (sin(x0) + x1 + ..., the badFirst family): its factoring rules run at every level; skipping them requires proving that all their comparisons fail and run no user code. It keeps the level by level treatment (and the known refusals of §9).
  • Two anchors, an anchor below a node with two crossed operands, a subtracted group, a negated call, a member access or a property getter (MutableGetter() when it is a property), a call of an instance method, a custom reflection object, any repetition among the other terms, a third constant.
  • Every chain under an explicit policy (the anchored chains are refused there, and their levels are recorded in a table of their own, FlattenAnchoredRefusals, consulted by the instances of such a policy only).
  • Derived simplifiers (the rule is private).

11.2 Representation

  • ExpressionMetadataTable (internal, Utils.Expressions): an open-addressing table by node reference, modeled on the effect table, held by the resource session (Session.SimplificationMetadata) and dropped by the outermost exit. An entry holds the raw hash, the key with the scope snapshot it was built under, and a state with the instance that recorded it.
  • SimplifiedExpressionInfo (internal record struct): Expression, StructuralHash, Key, State, assembled by ExpressionSimplifier.DescribeTerm for the next stages. Effects are not duplicated: the effect table of the session remains their only cache.
  • Accessors used by the canonicalizations: GetStructuralHash (the hash of GroupBy), GetCanonicalKey and GetCanonicalKeys (keys of terms, function arguments and factors). Opt-in counters: SimplificationMetadataStatistics.
  • What is stored. Every canonicalization reads the table. Only the canonicalizations whose values an enclosing one will read again store theirs: a level of the level by level treatment that already collected a canonicalized operand (more than two terms, or more than two factors). The single canonicalization of a flattened chain (linear or anchored), a two-term sum and a two-factor product store nothing: no enclosing canonicalization collects their terms again, and a first measure that stored everything cost a + b 856 bytes (+34 %) and linear 32 +55 % allocations for no reuse. A state is recorded only in a table the operation already holds, for the same reason. Values that are not stored are computed exactly as before.

Strategies compared: changing the engine signatures (PrepareExpression, TransformCore) to return an enriched value would break every derived transformer and add a parameter to the hot path (measured at about 10 ns per node in the dispatcher mission); an enriched TransformContext only lives during one node and never reaches the parent's canonicalization; a ConditionalWeakTable costs a dependent handle per node. A table of the operation, filled where the values are already computed and read where they were recomputed, changes no signature and no hot path.

11.3 Hash and key: validity and scope

  • Hash. StructuralHashRaw starts from an empty scope stack, identifies free parameters and unsupported nodes by identity and constants by value (safe constants only, no user code), so it is a function of the immutable node alone: a stored hash is valid for the whole operation, for every instance and policy. Equal structure implies equal hash; an equal hash is never taken as equality: the grouping still confirms it with StructuralEqualsRaw (pinned by a forged-collision test).
  • Key. A key encodes a parameter bound by an enclosing lambda by its position, so it depends on the scope frames. A stored key is reused only under a snapshot made of the very same frame arrays, in the same order; otherwise it is rebuilt (and replaces the entry). The reused key is the instance built before; keys are immutable values, so the order is unchanged.
  • Budgets. Computing a value consumes the structural visits of its walk; reusing it consumes none, because no walk happens. A value is stored only once fully computed, so a refusal in the middle of a walk leaves no entry. Transformation visits are unchanged.
  • Lifetime. One public operation: a Simplify call and the internal work joining it (recursion, rules, comparer probes, the comparer's own simplification). Every public operation, nested ones included, starts with an empty table: the session detaches the enclosing table when an operation is entered and restores it on exit, as the comparison memo is detached (S5 P9). A nested public call (user code calling Simplify or ExpressionComparer.Equals while an operation runs) therefore never reads its caller's entries and its own budget stays exact, which NestedPublicEquals_AppliesItsOwnStructuralBudget_WithoutTheRetainedMemo pins (a first version shared the table with nested calls and failed that test). Nothing is global.

Mission C3b prepares the global factoring of C3c: it gives each term of a chain a characterized representation (coefficient, factors, family, metadata), and widens the admission to a family of repeated chains that the new treatment handles at a lower cost without losing any historical simplification. 12.3 Phases C and D: metadata and classification

  • Present and valid: the hash and the key stored by a canonicalization of the running operation are read back, with no walk (GetStructuralHash, GetCanonicalKey).
  • Absent: computed once; stored only when the analysis is asked to record.
  • Key of another lexical context: never reused; only the key is rebuilt, the hash (scope-independent) is still reused.

12.4 Phase E: spine chains

A chain refused as linear for a repetition is a spine chain (IsSpineChain) when:

  1. it is a comb: every crossed node has at most one crossed operand;
  2. the local conditions of a linear chain hold, every term is a linear term other than the square x * x, and the chain holds at most two constant terms;
  3. the two terms of the deepest node form a linear chain (§3.2: no rule applies to them);
  4. above it, the sub-chain below every node holds two monomials whose coefficient is certainly not zero: all occurrences of the monomial (a parameter, an unordered pair of parameters, or the constant monomial when the sub-chain holds exactly one constant) have the same effective sign (sign of the term × sign of the coefficient), and, for an integral type, the sum of their magnitudes stays below 2^(bits - 1) (wrapping arithmetic could reach zero).

No rule applies above the deepest pair. By induction, the prepared operand from below is the canonical sum of the sub-chain, which has at least two groups (two certainly non-zero monomials cannot both cancel), hence an ordinary Add node; the other operand is a prepared linear term (a parameter, a product, a non-zero constant). The zero, constant and negation rules need a zero, two constants or a negation; the subtraction rules need a sum or a difference as their right operand (a subtracted group is refused); the logarithm and trigonometric rules need calls; the factoring rules augment the sum to 1 * sum and compare only 1, the sum, and the factors of the term (a parameter or a constant): a sum of two monomials with non-zero coefficients is equal neither to a parameter nor to a constant, whatever the comparer simplifies it to.

Treatment (FlattenSpineChain). Each term is prepared once (PrepareExpression, source order); the deepest sub-chain that is still linear is canonicalized once (it is a linear chain: §3); each level above it is its finalization, the canonicalization of the terms collected from its two prepared operands, the child going through the NaN fold of its parent's preparation. No composition of canonicalizations is assumed: every level is computed as level by level does it, so grouping, cancellation, constant aggregation and the stable order of tied keys are those of master by construction. What is removed is the rule dispatch of every level, the factor comparisons of the factoring rules (each of which simplified the sub-chain again through the comparer) and the admission walks of the sub-chains. Keys and hashes of the intermediate levels are stored for the next level (C3a), the root stores nothing.

Handoff. A spine chain only holds linear terms, so the rule cannot tell it from a linear chain by itself: the admission leaves its root in the session of the running operation (Session.AdmittedSpineRoot), which the flatten rule invoked right after it consumes (cleared on read, matched by reference). The root belongs to one public operation: Enter detaches it, so a nested operation neither sees nor consumes its caller's root, and Exit (in a finally) restores it, which also releases it when a budget refusal between the admission and the rule ends the operation (PR #701 review: a first version used a thread-static slot, which kept the root alive after such a refusal). Outside an operation the admission does not admit spine chains.

Known refusals. A refused spine test reports the spine index of its cause; the chain is recorded only down to the shallower of the linear and spine depths, so every recorded node is refused by every family.

Policies, derived simplifiers. The terms are inert, so the admission and the treatment are the same under every policy (validated, §12.6). A derived simplifier keeps the effect gate (no spine walk).

12.5 Phase F: contract with C3c

C3c obtains the analyzed terms of a chain (AdditiveTermAnalysis.Analyze), buckets them by factors (GroupByFactors, hash then structural confirmation), finds common factors (TryFindCommonFactor: a*x and 3*x share x), reads the coefficients (Coefficient, the original constant nodes) and merges them through the existing BuildFactoredProduct / AggregateFactoredCoefficient, without extracting anything again from the raw expressions. The tests show that x, 2*x, a*x, 3*x, x², 2*x² and sin(x), 3*sin(x) are brought together. The spine treatment is where such a merge will plug in: its levels are the places where the historical treatment leaves x + x unmerged.

12.8 Behaviors deliberately unchanged, residual limits

  • No result changes: the spine treatment computes each level as level by level; repeated terms are still grouped as x + x and not merged (C3c). x + x + x, 2*a + 2*b, cancellations down to one monomial, balanced trees, powers, calls, negations, three constants and subtracted groups keep the level by level treatment.

  • AdditiveTermAnalysis is not yet used by the production paths: it is the input of C3c. No Canonical state was added.

13. Global factoring of the additive chains (mission C3c)

Mission C3c uses the term analysis of C3b and the metadata of C3a to simplify a whole additive chain from its terms: the terms holding the same factors are merged, and the terms of a chain are grouped by their common factors, instead of a succession of binary factorings that only sees the pairs the grouping of the source happens to form. Unlike C3a and C3b, C3c produces new simplified forms (x^2 + 2*x + a*x + x + 15 + a + 3*cos(x) + 5*sin(x) gives 15 + (a + (3*cos(x) + (5*sin(x) + (x*(3 + a) + x^2)))) instead of keeping x, 2*x and a*x apart), within the symbolic-equivalence contract and the policies of the simplifier.

13.2 Phase A: identical monomials (MergeIdenticalMonomials)

In the additive canonicalization of the built-in type (CanonicalizeAdditiveTerms, so at every level and for every flattened chain), after the pinned terms are split off, the zeros dropped and the constants aggregated:

  1. the inert terms are analyzed once (AdditiveTermAnalysis.Analyze, hashes read from the metadata of C3a when a lower level stored them) and grouped by their factors (GroupByFactors: buckets by the order-independent factor hash, every member confirmed by a multiset comparison of the factors, StructuralEqualsRaw), so 2*a*b and 3*b*a meet and x*x never meets x;
  2. only the terms whose factor part is a parameter, a product, an ordinary power or a call of a runtime method merge; an opaque term (a division, a conversion, a custom operator, an extension node) keeps the historical grouping;
  3. the coefficients of a group (an implicit 1) are summed in source order with FoldConstants, as successive binary factorings fold them (SumCoefficients): exact modular arithmetic for the integral types (3*x - 4*x is 4294967295*x for uint, as before), IEEE-754 for the floating-point types (an overflow gives the infinity the binary factoring gives);
  4. the group becomes one term: the factor part of its first member (the right operand of the canonical c * rest, never rebuilt) times the sum; 1 gives the factors alone, -1 the factors subtracted, 0 drops the group (the terms are inert).

Coefficients are computed directly: 2*x + 3*x + 4*x gives 9*x, never (2 + 3)*x + 4*x. A derived simplifier keeps the historical canonicalization (MergesMonomials, internal and virtual for the level by level references of the tests only).

13.3 Phase B: common factors (FactorCommonFactors)

Only on all the terms of a whole chain (a factorable chain, §13.4, and recursively the residues of one), so that the choice of a factor never depends on how the source groups them:

  • Candidates: every factor of a mergeable term (compared structurally, as a multiset member) and every numeric coefficient other than 0, 1, -1 (compared by value; a constant term counts as its own coefficient, as the binary rule factors 2*a + 2). Each round indexes the candidates of every term once in two dictionaries (structural hash confirmed by a structural comparison, one structural visit per indexed factor): never all terms against all terms.
  • Choice: the candidate held by the most terms (it removes the most terms); a tie prefers a symbolic factor to a coefficient, then the first in the canonical order of the keys (ExpressionCanonicalOrder). The choice depends on the structure of the terms only, never on their source order nor on a "main" variable: a*b + a*c + b*c gives the same tree in the six orders.
  • Factoring: the terms holding the candidate become one product, the candidate times the canonical sum of their residues (each term without one occurrence of the factor, or without its coefficient), the residues being factored in turn and their constants aggregated once: 2*x + a*x + 3*x gives x*(5 + a) directly, a*b*c + a*b*d gives a*(b*(c + d)), a*x + a*y + b*x + b*y gives (x + y)*(a + b). The product is canonical (BuildCanonicalProduct, the core of the multiplicative canonicalization), its numeric factors folded into one coefficient; terms that are all subtracted give a subtracted product of a positive sum. Every round removes at least one term, so the rounds end with no candidate shared by two terms.

13.4 Admission: the factorable family (IsFactorableChain)

After the linear test refused a chain, inside an operation, for the built-in type: every crossed node is an ordinary operator of a flattened type and every term is factorable: a finite numeric constant, a parameter, an ordinary negation of a product, or an ordinary product of parameters, finite constants, ordinary powers of a parameter by a finite constant and calls of an inert runtime function of Math or double (Sin, Cos, Tan, their hyperbolic and inverse forms, Exp, Sqrt, Cbrt, Abs) on parameters and constants, one call per term at most. Refused, and so kept level by level:

  • a crossed node with two constant operands (the levels fold it exactly: -0.0 + -0.0 is -0.0);
  • a logarithm, Pow, a power of a call, two calls in one product (§13.1), a division, a conversion, a custom operator, a call that is not inert (an anchored chain, C2), any extension node or impersonating reflection object (type tests only, the provenance of a method checked before its name is read: ETC R4, S5 A-10);
  • a factor that is a sum (a + 3 + (a + 3)*x: the levels factor the sub-sum a + 3 itself, (1 + x)*(3 + a));
  • a shared sub-chain whose revisits exceed its distinct nodes (a DAG of 2^n paths): refused after a number of visits linear in the distinct nodes. A few shared occurrences are accepted, so that a tree and its rebuilt copy are simplified alike (the comparer's reflexivity relies on it).

The family includes the linear terms, so it supersedes the spine chains of C3b, which are tested only when it is switched off (ExpressionSimplifier.FactorableChainsDisabled, audits and tests; the C1/C2/C3b test classes run with it, against a level by level reference that merges monomials too). A refused chain records its spine down to the shallower of the linear and factorable depths (C1 known refusals). The admitted root travels to the rule in the session (ExpressionResourceBudget.Session.AdmittedFactorableRoot), detached by a nested operation and released on exit, as the spine root after the review of #701.

13.5 The signature rules of an admitted chain

Level by level, a crossed node sees two terms, a term and a canonical sub-sum, or two sub-sums. The zero, constant, negation and reassociation rules are identities the canonicalization applies. The factoring rules split products at their top node and compare the parts; a sub-sum is a single augmented factor, and since no factor of a factorable term is a sum, a sub-sum can only match a whole sub-sum, and two terms match on a factor or a coefficient: phase A and phase B find these among all the terms at once. The logarithm and trigonometric rules cannot apply (§13.4). So no applicable rule is lost: the global result applies every identity the levels apply, among all the terms instead of the pairs the source's grouping forms. Nothing calls the dispatcher level by level: the guarantee comes from the admission, not from a fallback.

13.6 Canonical shapes

  • Factored products: the binary factoring of the built-in type builds the canonical product when the coefficient is not a constant (a*x + b*x gives x*(a + b), the tree a second call gave from (a + b)*x), the shape phase B builds.
  • Subtracted sums: a factorable chain whose terms are all subtracted gives the negation of their sum, as SubstractionWithZero (0 - t → -t) and SubstractionWithNegate ((-a) - b → -(a + b)) give it; a lone subtracted constant is folded (0 - 1 → -1, in every type); an unsigned type keeps 0 - t.
  • 0 - t stays the representation of a subtracted group inside a sum (S5 P10), except for a constant of a type with an ordinary negation, which is folded into its negation in every canonicalization of the built-in type (y - 1 gives -1 + y, the form the next call folded from (0 - 1) + y; NegateSubtractedConstant); an unsigned type kept 0 - c until mission C3d, which folds it to its modular value too (§14.3, F6).

13.7 Idempotence

Simplify(Simplify(e)) is structurally Simplify(e) on every example of the mission and on 300 random factorable chains (ExpressionSimplifierGlobalFactoringTests); no state is promoted to Canonical. The transformations are oriented: phase B only factors (the simplifier has no distribution of a product over a sum), and every round removes terms, so no a*x + b*x → (a + b)*x → a*x + b*x cycle exists. A result holding a factor that is a sum is not factorable on the second pass; it is simplified level by level, where no rule applies to it, except the documented limits of §13.12.

13.8 Complexity and budgets

  • Phase A: one analysis per canonicalization (hashes reused across levels through C3a), bucketed grouping, linear expected.
  • Phase B: per round, the factors are indexed once (one structural visit each, so the rounds are bounded by the structural budget); a round removes at least one term; the residues are canonicalized recursively over fewer factors.
  • A factorable chain is prepared once per term and canonicalized once: repeated chains no longer finalize each level (tail 512, §13.10). The admission walk is iterative, as the result's final test: a 100,000-term chain is simplified on a 256 KiB thread. Budgets are never bypassed: every walk consumes its structural visits.
  • The caches P3/P9 and the probes of FactorEqualityProbe are untouched.

13.12 Behaviors deliberately unchanged, residual limits

  • Phase B only factors the factors present in the terms: it never recognizes a sum as a factor of another term, which is why a term holding a sum refuses the chain (x + y + a*(x + y) keeps the levels, whose binary rule gives (1 + a)*(x + y)); powers are never split (x^2 + x*y stays).
  • A coefficient produced by the binary factoring on a non-factorable chain was still left for the next pass (x*y + (1 - x)*y gave y*(x + (1 + (0 - x))), S5 P14); since mission C3d it is canonical and the product is y (§14.3).
  • A shared sub-chain beyond the revisit allowance, a chain holding a non-inert call, a division, a logarithm or a constant pair keeps the level by level treatment (with phase A at each level).
  • Derived simplifiers keep their historical results; the factorable family and phase A are reserved to the built-in type.
  • Free parameters of one type still tie in the canonical order (S4): a sum of free parameters may keep an order that depends on the source; bound parameters order by their scope.

14. Stabilization, idempotence and performance (mission C3d)

Mission C3d starts from master b7ab5917 (C3c merged) and develops no new factoring family. Its goal is that one public call returns the tree a second call returns, wherever the existing rules allow it without changing any permitted behavior, and that the allocations C3c added go down. The baseline is a separate worktree of b7ab5917; every figure below compares the branch with it, never the branch with itself.

14.1 Audit tool: the stability campaign (C3d.1)

tests/ExpressionSimplifierAudit gains the command stability (Campaigns.Stability.cs). For every case:

  1. E, S(E), S(S(E)), … are computed up to a fixed point, a cycle, an error or eight passes; forms are compared by the strict structural signature of the harness (StrictForm), never by a hash.
  2. When one pass does not stabilize, the smallest subtree of S(E) that the second pass rewrites is located by a parallel walk of both trees (the residue), and the second pass is traced: the first rule whose output differs structurally from its input is the responsible rule.
  3. The family of the case is the responsible rule and the shapes of the residue before and after, leaves abstracted (parameters renamed by first occurrence, constants other than 0, 1, -1, 2 printed c).
  4. The first cases of each family are reduced: a node is replaced by a child, a parameter or the constant 0, 1 or 2 of its own type, while the case keeps its kind (cycle or not stable) and its responsible rule. Every candidate is rebuilt by the framework factories, so the reduction never changes a type or an operator.

Campaigns.RunStability takes any case source and simplifier, so the external harness ran it on the differential corpus of C3b/C3c (40,000 cases, default policy; 15,000 per explicit policy and for a derived simplifier). On b7ab5917 it reproduces the documented baseline exactly: 63,399 cases stable after one pass and 100 cycles on converge, 6,190 non-idempotent cases on the corpus.

14.3 Corrections (C3d.2)

All the corrections apply to the built-in type only (StabilizesRebuiltForms, which follows MergesMonomials, so that the level by level references of the tests match the built-in type); derived simplifiers keep the historical rules.

  • F1, finished reassociations. SubstractionWithAddition and SubstractionWithSubstraction transform the two nodes they rebuild from their prepared operands (FinishReassociation, through TransformCoreWithPreparedOperands: rules and finalization, one transformation visit each, no second preparation). One finishing step, not a loop.
  • F2, negated sums. A canonical sum whose inert terms are all subtracted, in a type with an ordinary negation, is the negation of their sum (IsNegatedSum): the form SubstractionWithZero and SubstractionWithNegate give.
  • F3, finished nested divisions. DivisionOfDivision transforms the division it builds (FinishDivision).
  • F4, normalized products. The canonical product moves the ordinary negations of its inert factors out and multiplies its inert constant factors once (NormalizeProductFactors; a resulting 1 is dropped, -1 becomes the sign, 0 is kept); the commutation t * c → c * t and the two constant combinations transform the product they build when it is one the next call rewrote (FinishProduct).
  • F5, canonical coefficients. The coefficient of a binary factoring is the canonical sum of the terms of both coefficients (CanonicalizeFactoredCoefficient), negated as a whole when all its terms are subtracted (the sign the levels and the global factoring give such a group); a constant coefficient of a constant factor is folded. Only a coefficient that holds a subtraction, a negation or two constants is canonicalized (HasAdditiveNegation, CountAdditiveConstants, both without allocation): a sum of added terms keeps the historical node, since canonicalizing a coefficient that grows by one term per level made the dag family quadratic in allocations (§14.8).
  • F6, unsigned subtracted constants. The subtracted constant of an unsigned canonical sum is folded to its modular value (p - 9ul gives 18446744073709551607 + p), as SubstractionOfConstants folded (0 - 9) + p at the next call. This reverses a C3c choice (which kept 0 - c for readability, a form no call ever left alone).

Guards found by the test suites and kept:

  • The finishing re-entries run only on inert, finite arithmetic (FinishesRebuilt, IsFinishableOperand): on an opaque constant, a call, a member access or a decimal, the re-entered rules compared operands and added user Equals calls and comparer simplifications the S5 P15/P16 tests pin; on a NaN or an infinity they anticipated the policy-dependent NaN fold. Those cases keep the nodes as built.
  • Decimal sums and products are never reassociated (their operators throw on overflow).
  • FinishProduct finishes only the shapes the next call rewrote; finishing a plain c * t re-ran the comparing rules.

14.4 Representation invariants

  1. Factors: inert factors in canonical key order, pinned factors after them in source order; numeric constant factors of one type folded into one, first; ordinary negations moved out of the product (-(c * a), the fixed point of MultiplicationWithNegate).
  2. Terms: inert terms in canonical order (ties of free parameters keep their collection order, S4), pinned terms after them; right-associated.
  3. Numeric coefficient: on the left of the product (c * a).
  4. Implicit coefficients: 1 never written; -1 written as a negation or a subtracted term.
  5. Zero coefficients: the term is dropped; a zero constant factor is kept until CanDropOperand allows the rules to drop the other factors.
  6. Negations: a subtracted term inside a sum is 0 - t; a sum whose terms are all subtracted is -(sum); a product is -(product).
  7. Subtractions: a subtracted constant is folded into a negative (signed types) or modular (unsigned types) constant.
  8. Factored products: the canonical product of the factor and of the canonical coefficient.

No result is promoted to CanonicalizationState.Canonical: the fixed point is measured, not proven, and the residual families of §14.7 show it does not hold everywhere.

14.5 Optimizations (C3d.3)

  • O1. FactorCommonFactors builds its monomials from the AdditiveTermAnalysis that MergeIdenticalMonomials computed for the same terms (FactoringMonomial.FromAnalysis); only the merged terms are decomposed again.
  • O3. GroupByFactors chains the representatives of a hash bucket through an index array instead of one list per bucket.
  • Not done: a dedicated path for very small sums (x+x+x keeps its 3.64 KB, the analysis arrays): the measured gain did not justify a second merging path. The repeated per-round dictionaries of FindBestCandidate stay (one round in every measured family but wide, §14.8).

14.7 Residual cycles and non-idempotence, and why

  • Free-parameter ties (304 corpus cases, 256 cycles; 16 converge cycles). Two distinct free parameters of one type have no canonical order (S4, documented in ExpressionCanonicalOrder); the stable sort keeps their collection order, and a second call that takes the level path collects them in another order. Ordering them by name would remove the cycles but would invent an order the S4 design deliberately refuses; this is left to a decision.
  • Admission-dependent factoring (≈ 470 corpus cases). A constant pair in the source ((p0*p1) + (p0 + (2 + 0))) or a term with a sum factor refuses the factorable family, so phase B does not run; the second call sees 2 + (p0 + p0*p1), admits it and factors p0. Admitting constant pairs would revisit the exactness decisions of C3c (signed zeros).
  • Finishing left out on purpose. Calls (Log, Sin), opaque constants, decimals and non-finite constants keep their nodes as built (§14.3); the NaN family stays with the policy.
  • Specialized rules (MultiplicationOfEqualsElements, the reciprocal powers): outside C3, deferred.
  • AdditionOfCos2andSin2Number throws NullReferenceException on x^x + x^y (978 converge errors, pre-existing): a one-line guard, deferred to a separate fix.
  • Pre-existing explosion outside C3: a subtraction chain of division terms (1 - x1/x0 - x2/x0 - …, never admitted) costs 7.7 ms at 16 terms and 1.4 s at 64 on b7ab5917 as on the branch (§14.9).

The simplifier is therefore not globally idempotent or canonical.

15.2 Root cause of the NullReferenceException

AdditionOfCos2andSin2Number read left.Right as ConstantExpression and then .Value without testing the cast: any exponent that is not a constant (a parameter, a call, a computed expression) dereferenced null. The rule is a candidate for every sum of two ordinary powers, so the defect hit every such sum, and the comparer, which simplifies first.

15.3 Correction of the trigonometric rules

  • Shape before value. The identity checks every shape before reading any value: two ordinary powers of one type, both exponents the double constant 2 (IsSquareExponent), both bases calls of the sine and the cosine of that type with one argument each, then the structural identity of the arguments. Any other shape declines (null), never throws.
  • Exact function of the type. TryGetTrigonometricArgument (and TryMatchTrigonometricPair for the quotients and products) accepts a call only when it is exactly the public static T F(T) function of the call's own result type, one argument, its provenance checked first (ExpressionReflectionProvenance.IsRuntime, IsSameMember, S5 A-10): an overload of another arity, a homonym with other values (a degree-based sine) or an impersonating reflection object never matches. TrigonometricMethods resolves null for a function the type does not declare with that signature and return type (an explicit interface implementation), and the rule then declines instead of building a call of double.
  • Tests: ExpressionSimplifierTrigonometricRuleTests (non-constant exponents, other constant exponents, other arguments or functions, foreign methods called directly or through a derived simplifier, extension operands, effectful arguments, the identities that still apply) and ExpressionTrigonometricRuleSecurityTests (impersonating methods declined without being read; mixed calls keep the impersonated evaluation). The deep-audit test S5A01_…_Declines pins the fix.

15.4 Root cause of the cost of the quotient chains

A chain 1 - x1/x0 - … - xn/x0 was refused by the linear family (a division is not a linear term, §12) and simplified level by level. At every level SubstractionOfEqualsElements compares the canonical prefix P_k with the new quotient and the constant; the comparer simplifies P_k again, and that simplification rebuilds the prefix level by level through the 0 - t re-entries of the sign rules. The work is cubic in n, and 97 % of the transformation visits ran inside comparer simplifications: for 64 quotients, 373,830 transformation visits, 25.8 million structural visits and 345,408 comparer simplifications (2.4 s).

15.5 Correction: linear quotients

A linear chain (IsLinearChain, §12) of double or float may now hold linear quotients (IsLinearQuotient): an ordinary division of a parameter by a parameter, of the chain's type. LinearChainOperands keeps its proof obligations:

  • the numerators are parameters that the chain holds nowhere else (as a term, a factor or another numerator);
  • a denominator may divide several quotients (_denominators) but is never a term, a factor or a numerator;
  • every other condition of the linear family is unchanged (constants, coefficients, crossed nodes, subtracted groups).

Why the result is the level by level result. No rule prepares such a quotient into anything else (no constant, no negation, no nested division, no call: a field division of two parameters never throws in IEEE-754). Two operands of a level always hold a parameter the other does not hold (a term, a factor or a numerator), the simplifier never introduces a parameter and never removes one from such a chain, so the factoring comparisons of AdditionOfEqualsElements and SubstractionOfEqualsElements fail at every level and nothing cancels, exactly as for the parameters of a linear chain. The accepted chain is therefore rebuilt canonically without any comparison: structurally the tree the levels give (pinned by ExpressionSimplifierFlattenTests.QuotientChains_AreAccepted_AndGiveTheLevelByLevelResult against a derived simplifier).

Refused on purpose, and kept level by level (OtherQuotientChains_AreDeclined_AndKeepTheLevelByLevelResult): integral quotients (truncating, throwing on a zero divisor), decimal chains, a quotient whose numerator or denominator is not a parameter, a denominator that is also a term, a factor or a numerator, a repeated numerator. The spine family (§12.4) keeps its linear terms only (IsSpineTerm): a quotient is never a spine term.

Tests: ExpressionSimplifierDivisionChainTests (values in every type and shape, one result under every policy, effectful chains keep their evaluations, derived simplifiers keep their rules, budgets refuse cleanly at the limit, shared quotient instances treated once, a 2,000-term chain on a small stack, concurrency and re-entrancy, linear work).

The built-in simplifier itself is the reference of every differential test: ExpressionSimplifier.GroupedQuotientChainsDisabled (internal, per thread, audits and tests only) switches the new family off and leaves every other path as it is, so the simplifier treats such a chain level by level, as before. A derived simplifier is not the reference (its plan differs since C3c, and it keeps the level by level treatment anyway).

16.3 Admission: IsGroupedLinearQuotientChain

FlattenAdmission tests the family only when the linear walk refused the chain for a subtracted group (its first refusal cause is SubtractedGroup), after the factorable family refused it too, for the exact built-in type, inside an operation. Every other linear refusal is a refusal of this family as well (its conditions are those of a linear chain, with at most one constant and no anchor), so no other chain is walked once more. The conditions:

  1. the chain is double or float;
  2. every crossed node is an ordinary Add or Subtract of that type (AsCrossedAdditiveNode, IsOrdinaryBinaryArithmetic): no custom, lifted or checked operator (a checked node is not crossed, so it is a term, and no term is one);
  3. no crossed node has two constant operands;
  4. every term is a linear term (a parameter, a finite non-zero constant, a product of a parameter by a parameter or by a finite constant other than 0, 1, -1) or a linear quotient (IsLinearQuotient: an ordinary division of a parameter by a parameter of the chain's type, §15.5): no call, invocation, member access, conversion, negation, nested division, extension or dynamic node;
  5. the checks of LinearChainOperands over the whole tree, every branch included: a numerator occurs once and is never a term, a factor or another numerator; a denominator may divide several quotients but is never a term, a factor or a numerator; the coefficients differ from each other and from the constant term;
  6. at most one constant term (LinearChainOperands.ForGroupedChain, explicit local state) and at least one quotient (HasQuotients; a chain of linear terms only belongs to the factorable family).

The walk is the walk of IsLinearChain without the subtracted-group and anchor cases: iterative, one structural visit per crossed node, type tests first (an extension node is never read; the operator of a node is classified only once both operands are known to be framework nodes of an accepted shape), the pending stack allocated only for a branched tree. A shared sub-chain holds a numerator twice (refused at its second occurrence) or constants only (refused at its first constant pair), so the walk is linear in the distinct nodes even on a DAG whose paths multiply.

Refusal propagation. A refusal of the family is inherited by every chain that contains its cause, as for the linear family (§9): a local cause by every chain that crosses its node, a repetition by every chain that holds both occurrences, a second constant by every chain that holds both constants. RecordRefusedSpine (now given its target table and returning the depth it proved) records the spine nodes that contain the cause in the family's own table, FlattenGroupedRefusals, which only ever skips this test (a node below a refused grouped chain may still be a linear chain). The shared table of known refusals is lowered to the depth this family proved, so that a sub-chain the family may admit is never refused by one lookup; the linear refusal depth is not reused unproven. The type of the chain and the absence of quotients (NoQuotient) are inherited by every sub-chain and do not restrict that depth. The old paths are unchanged for the chains no family admits: the linear walk, then the factorable walk (or the spine test when the factorable family is switched off), with their own causes and depths.

16.4 Treatment: FlattenGroupedQuotientChain

The admitted root is recorded in the session (AdmittedGroupedQuotientRoot, detached by every nested operation and restored on exit, like the spine and factorable roots); the flatten rule invoked right after it on that root consumes it. FlattenedChain.Extract already gives the signed terms of the whole tree, the inverted sign applied through every subtracted group: the rule prepares each term once, in source order (PrepareExpression, RecordLocallySimplified), collects them and canonicalizes them once with AdditiveFactoring.None. Each quotient is an indivisible additive atom: IsFactorableTerm, AdditiveTermFamily and the algorithms of C3c/C3d are unchanged, and no transformation a/b + c/b → (a + c)/b, a/b - a/b → 0 or c * (a/b) → (c * a)/b is introduced. ExpressionTranformer.Flatten.cs is not modified.

Fallback. The proof below needs every prepared term to be its raw term, a constant node holding the same boxed value, or the same quotient or product (IsPreparedGroupedTerm). No rule is known to rewrite such a term; should one do so, the levels run one by one on the prepared terms, children before their parent, through TransformLevel and the NaN fold of the parent's preparation (TransformGroupedLevelByLevel, an explicit stack of frames, one structural visit per node): the historical treatment, without preparing anything twice. FlattenAdmissionStatistics.ForceGroupedFallback forces it for audits; the seeded corpus gives the historical structure through it too.

16.5 Why the result is the level by level result

Level by level, a crossed node sees two prepared operands, each a prepared term or the canonical form of a sub-chain.

  • Terms. Preparing a term gives the term itself (§15.5): a parameter, the constant, a product, a field division of two parameters, which never throws in IEEE-754; no rule rewrites it into a sum, a negation, a zero or a call.
  • The rules that apply are the reassociation rules on a subtracted canonical sum (SubstractionWithAddition, SubstractionWithSubstraction and their finishing), the sign rules on the symbolic 0 - t of a canonical subtracted term (SubstractionWithZero(0, t), AdditionWithNegate, SubstractionWithNegate) and the finalization, which canonicalizes the signed terms it collects. They only move signs and regroup terms: identities the global canonicalization applies.
  • Nothing else can apply. Two operands of a level always hold a parameter the other does not hold (a term, a factor or a numerator; a shared denominator is never an operand by itself), the simplifier never introduces a parameter and never removes one from such a chain, so every factoring comparison of AdditionOfEqualsElements, SubstractionOfEqualsElements and of the additive canonicalization fails and nothing cancels or merges; a sub-sum only matches another sub-sum (§13.4), never one with disjoint parameters. With one constant term no constant pair is folded; the only zeros are the symbolic ones of 0 - t, which the sign rules consume. No logarithm, power or call is present, so no other additive rule matches.
  • Order. Free parameters tie in the canonical order (S4); every rewrite above keeps the relative order of the terms of one sign, those of the left operand before those of the right one, so the per-level stable sorts compose into the stable sort of the signed terms in source order, which is what the single canonicalization sorts.
  • Effects and policies. Every operand is inert ordinary floating-point arithmetic over parameters and primitive constants: nothing throws, nothing calls user code, the effect gate admits every rule on it under every policy, the result does not depend on the policy, and no evaluation is dropped, repeated or moved.

This is the proof sketch; the equality is pinned on every tested shape against the historical treatment of the built-in simplifier, by strict structural identity after one pass (node types, static types, operator methods, constant bits and parameter instances), never through a numerical tolerance alone (§16.6).

16.9 Residual limits

  • The family is restricted: at most one constant term (with two, the level by level constant folding depends on the grouping), no repeated numerator, no denominator used elsewhere, no quotient of another shape (a constant or product numerator, a sum or product denominator, a nested division), no call, no negated term, double and float only.   • The structural visits grow as n log n, like those of every linear chain: the sort of the canonicalization (§15.7).

17.3 Why a local rule is not enough (strategy A, measured)

The experiment of the 2026-11-04 audit was reproduced in a separate worktree: a rule NegateOfNegate(Negate; Negate) -> operand.Operand, admitted by IsOrdinaryUnaryNegate on both nodes. On the 70,470 cases of the current converge corpus it changes 232 first-pass forms (229 historically), terminate 11, diff 34, effects 16, s3 5, raises the cases fixed after two calls from 272 to 423 (exactly the historical count) and gives 3 violations of the comparer invariant Equals(a, b) == Equals(S(a), S(b)) (none on the base).

Root cause of both: an unstable canonical form, not the comparer. The rule only sees the negations of the source and the ones a rule transforms itself (SubstractionWithZero, SubstractionWithNegate). The rules marked no above return the negation they build without transforming it, so its -(-t) waits for the next call: 148 cases keep the same first pass but move from fixed@1 to fixed@2. The three violations are such pairs: (-y) * ((y * -1) + ((1 - y) * -1)) (int and long, seed 1519) is -(-y) after one call (MultiplicationWithNegate over (-y) * (-1)) and y after two, so Equals(a, y) compares -(-y) with y (false) while Equals(S(a), S(y)) compares y with y (true); the double case -1 * (-1) against a product of powers of x ** 0 is the same with a constant. The comparison memo, the caches and the scopes play no part: the comparer simplifies once with the public simplifier, which is simply not idempotent there. Changing the comparer would have hidden the symptom; making the first call finish the negations removes it.

17.6 Mechanism and proof obligations

  • Finalization. FinalizeExpression of an ordinary Negate whose prepared operand is an ordinary Negate returns the operand of the latter. It runs where the additive canonicalization runs: after the rules, for every runtime type (a derived simplifier included, like the additive canonicalization), behind no effect gate.
  • Builder. BuildNegation(t) returns t.Operand when t is an ordinary negation, Expression.Negate(t) otherwise. Every negation the simplifier builds goes through it (x * -1, -1 * x, x / -1, (-a) * b, a * (-b), (-a) / b, a / (-b), the negated sums of the canonicalization and of the factoring, the negated products): the simplifier never builds -(-t). The negations built through TransformCore are finalized by the first point.
  • Admission. IsOrdinaryUnaryNegate on both nodes: the predefined, non-lifted, unchecked negation of a type of Types.Number that has one, whose method is absent or is the runtime operator itself (provenance checked first, A-10). Excluded: NegateChecked (outer or inner: checked(-(-int.MinValue)) throws), lifted int? negations, user operators (-(-s) runs the operator twice), impersonated reflection objects, a conversion between the two negations, unsigned types (no negation), extension nodes (never negations).
  • Exactness. Two ordinary negations are the identity on every value: float/double flip the sign bit twice (signed zeros, infinities, subnormals and NaN payloads bit for bit), signed integers wrap twice (-(-int.MinValue) is int.MinValue, same for short and long), decimal negation flips the sign without rounding, scale change or overflow. Neither can throw; the type of t is the type of the node.
  • Effects and policies. The two operators evaluate nothing but t: t is evaluated exactly once at the same place, every exception it raises is unchanged, so no evaluation is dropped, repeated, merged or moved, whatever t is (pure, reading or writing state, throwing, allocating, unknown, declared deterministic). The removal therefore needs none of the permissions of ExpressionBehaviorPermissions and is the same under every policy (default, Verified, TrustDeclarations, AggressiveSimplification, every ExceptionPreservation): -(-f()) gives f() for an observable f(), as a + -(-f()) already gave a + f() through the pinned terms of the canonicalization. No policy cache changes.
  • Resources. Two type tests and a provenance check per finalized or built negation, no walk: no new structural visit, no new transformation visit, no allocation (one node less per removed pair).

21.2 Questions of the admission (phase B of the mission)

  1. The admission is evaluated before the preparation of the operands, on the raw root, once per chain root.
  2. A refused chain can become admissible without any change of its operators: its refusing terms are folded by the levels (constant pairs, negated constants, products by 1), and no part of the engine tests the result again.
  3. Recorded refusals stay valid: the tables of known refusals (C1) hold raw nodes of the operation, which are never rebuilt; a result is a new tree and is never looked up.
  4. A sub-chain is not distinguished from a whole chain by the admission: every crossed node is offered as a root of its own, and an admitted sub-chain of a refused chain is factored on its own (M4, and the probe of case C).
  5. A parent does not reuse the analysis of its children: each canonicalization analyzes its terms again (with the hashes of C3a).
  6. The session cannot carry a new admission without keeping a decision that a rebuild invalidates: the admitted roots are consumed by the rule invoked right after the admission (§13.4).
  7. Factoring once per chain needs the boundary of the chain: the node no node of the same chain is transforming.

  • Same identities as the next call.   • No admission is widened. Every proof of §13.4 and §13.5 holds on the tested result: every term inert and factorable, every crossed node an ordinary operator, no constant pair crossed. 22.2 The boundary of a suffix

The result of the levels is CanonicalizeAdditiveTerms → BuildAdditiveExpression → BuildRightAssociative: a right spine g0 + (g1 + (… + gn)), each gi a group (a term, 0 - t, or a small sum of one grouping class). Its properties:

  • The suffix the next call factors is the longest suffix of factorable groups. The next call offers the root, then each right operand top-down; a suffix that holds a refused group is refused, so the first admitted suffix is the longest one whose groups are all factorable. A suffix admitted above it by another family is linear or anchored, and then so is every sub-chain; the only other family, the grouped quotient chains (§16), starts at a subtraction of a crossed operand, which a canonical spine never holds, and which stops the search.
  • Identifiable in one pass. Walking the spine bottom-up, the first group that is not factorable ends the suffix: every group and every spine node is tested once (no walk per suffix).
  • Syntactic boundary, semantic proof. The boundary only selects the candidate; the proof is the factorable admission of the candidate itself (IsFactorableChain, §13.5), so no quotient, pinned term or non-inert call is ever a factor.
  • No reassociation of the refused part. Only the suffix is canonicalized with common factors; the prefix is run through its own levels, as on the next call.
  • Convergence. Every association of the same terms gives one canonical result, so one suffix (21.5 and the matrix of ExpressionSimplifierRefusedSuffixFactoringTests), up to the free-parameter ties of S4.
  • Once. The suffix is factored at the boundary only (§21.3); its prefix levels run with the refused-chain marker set, so no node they build is finished again.

FinishRefusedChain now loops: the whole-chain test of M1, then, when the result stays refused, FinishRefusedSuffix, at most two rounds (the corpus needs one; the bound keeps the work linear whatever the prefix rebuilds).

  1. Search. The right spine is collected iteratively (one structural visit per node); a subtraction of a crossed operand stops it. Bottom-up, IsFactorableSuffixNode tests each node: no constant pair, its left group (and the last right group) factorable (IsFactorableTerm, or IsFactorableChain for a crossed group), an ordinary operator, type tests first (ETC R4).
  2. Candidate. The longest such proper suffix; IsLinearChain (the linear rule would change nothing) and IsFactorableChain (the admission the next call applies, DAG revisits included) decide on it.
  3. Change. The terms of the suffix come out of a canonicalization of the levels (identical monomials merged, constants aggregated), so only a common factor can remove one: their factoring candidates are indexed once (HasFactoringCandidate, the index of FindBestCandidate, no key and no sort), and a suffix without a shared candidate stops here. Otherwise its terms are canonicalized with common factors as FlattenFactorableChain does. A suffix of fewer than two non-zero terms, or whose factoring keeps every term (no common factor, or only another sign representation such as (0 - a) + (0 - b) as -(a + b)), returns the historical result: the prefix levels would rebuild it.
  4. Prefix. The left operands of the prefix are prepared again in source order, the marker set; then, bottom-up, each prefix node runs its additive signature rules through the group's dispatcher and its finalization (TransformLevel), on top of the NaN-folded result below. This is exactly the treatment the next call gives those nodes (the reference campaign checks it on every corpus).

Safety. Types: every rebuilt node is built by the factoring or by the rules of the levels. Evaluations: the factored suffix holds inert factorable terms only (parameters, finite constants, ordinary products, at most one inert runtime call of the allow-list per term), so no evaluation is moved, merged or dropped beyond what the factorable family already admits; quotients, calls that are not inert and every pinned term (placed last by the canonicalization, ETC R1b) stay outside the suffix; the prefix is treated by the rules under the effect gate. Exceptions: a quotient that may throw (an integral division by a parameter) is pinned, so it ends the spine and nothing follows it (IntegralTypes_KeepTheDivisionByZero_AndFactorExactly). Custom, checked and lifted operators are not ordinary, so they refuse the suffix; conversions are no factor. Signed zeros, NaN and infinities: a non-finite constant refuses the family, and the identities are those of C3c (exact on integers and decimals — decimal chains are not canonicalized at all —, the reassociation latitude of S3 on floating point, 22.5). Policies: the factorable family is admitted under every policy with the same terms; Verified and TrustDeclarations see the same inert terms. ETC and R4: type tests and runtime reflection only (SuffixSearch_NeverReadsHostileNodes); a prefix holding an impersonating call is prepared as the next call prepares it, never invoked. Budgets: every walk counts its visits and every level its transformation visit; a refusal leaves the marker restored (finally).

Rules of the levels. No historical rule is bypassed: the suffix is only replaced by its factored form when the factorable family admits it, the family whose global factoring applies every identity the additive rules apply on such terms (§13.5: AdditionOfEqualsElements, SubstractionOfEqualsElements, SubstractionWithAddition, SubstractionWithSubstraction, the negation, zero and constant identities; the logarithm, trigonometric, power and quotient rules never match a factorable term pair), and the prefix runs every rule of its levels.

23.2 Audit of the handlers

  • AdditionOfEqualsElements decomposes each operand into the factors of an ordinary top-level product, or 1 * t (the augmentation); it declines when an augmented operand is the constant 1 (AUD-03) and on a constant pair (S5A-04). It compares at most four pairs with one FactorEqualityProbe (the comparer's simplification of each candidate, computed at most once), in a fixed order (leading, then crossed, then trailing), swaps the factors accordingly and builds BuildFactoredProduct. Two equal products give BuildCanonicalProduct(2, factors) (S5A-05). The coefficient is folded when both are constants (TryFoldFactoredCoefficient), canonicalized when both are single terms (M2, CanonicalizeFactoredCoefficient), aggregated otherwise; a symbolic coefficient gives the canonical product of its factors and of the factor (C3c).
  • SubstractionOfEqualsElements: the same decomposition and comparisons; the cancellation of two equal coefficients gives the zero of the type; no IsConstantPair guard (the constant pair is folded by SubstractionOfConstants first).
  • Exceptions and effects. Both run under the effect gate of the dispatcher: a pinned term (an observable call, an integral division by a parameter, which may throw) is never grouped by the canonicalization, so it never forms a pair or a group that reaches them from the finishing; a quotient by a constant is inert (x / 3 is merged with itself: t + t = 2 * t is exact in modular arithmetic, and the doubling of a floating-point value is exact).
  • The comparer. FactorEqualityProbe simplifies each candidate through ExpressionComparer.SimplifyForComparison (the P3 cache, the P9 memo, the P15 observed memo); a simplification made for a probe is not finished by mission B (23.4), so that the decisions of the rules stay those of master. Raw structural equality implies the comparer's equality, so the pre-test never claims a factor the rules would refuse to see, and the decision stays the rules'.

23.4 Mechanism and proof obligations

FinishRefusedChain (§21, §22) now runs, in each of its at most two rounds, the whole-chain test (M1), the suffix finishing (M4), and, when neither changes the result, FinishRefusedBinaryPair:

  1. Gate. Not inside a comparer simplification (ComparisonMemoDepth > 0), only for the flattened chain types (double, float, int, long, uint, ulong: decimal, short and lifted chains are never canonicalized), only for the built-in simplifier inside an operation.
  2. Spine. The right spine, as in M4 (a subtraction of a crossed operand stops it); a last 0 - t is a term of the node above. A spine of more than 512 nodes is not searched: its candidate and prefix could not both pass the bounds of step 4.
  3. Pre-test, bottom-up, first hit. A node whose operands are opaque, or whose operator is not ordinary, stops the search (type tests first, ETC R4). A node is a candidate when its left operand, or, for the last node, its right operand, is a group (two or more non-zero terms) whose first two terms share a factor, or when the last node's two operands share a factor. Sharing is decided as the rules decompose (0 - t and -t removed, an ordinary product or 1 * t), by raw structural equality (ExpressionComparer.StructuralEqualsRaw, no user code) of the same instance (not opaque) or of two trees of at most 32 nodes. No pair holding a NaN, infinite or negative zero floating-point constant is a candidate (23.5).
  4. Candidate and prefix. The candidate holds at most 256 nodes, and the left operands of the prefix, which are prepared again, at most 256 together (repetitions counted), all made only of parameters, constants, framework unary and binary nodes and static calls whose operators and methods are runtime reflection objects (S5 A-10): no impersonating method or extension node is ever rebuilt by the finishing, a shared DAG whose paths multiply is never prepared again (the first version re-ran a prefix of such DAGs and hung M4's ExplosiveDag_IsRefusedAfterLinearWork), and the depth of the re-run stays bounded (a prefix of 512 parameters exhausted a 256 KiB stack where the levels succeed).
  5. Treatment. The candidate is transformed again with the refused-chain marker set (PrepareExpression: admission, rules, finalization, exactly the next call's treatment of that crossed node). An unchanged node keeps the historical result; otherwise the prefix nodes are run again on top of it (RunPrefixLevels, shared with M4: left operands prepared again, then TransformLevel with the group's dispatcher).

Safety. No identity is added: every node is rebuilt by the rules, the admission and the finalization of the engine, under the effect gate and the resource budget, on the operands the next call prepares. IsFactorableChain is unchanged: a quotient or a sum is a factor of the binary rules only, never of the global factoring. Types: every node comes out of the engine's own factories. Evaluations and exceptions: a pinned term is never grouped and never in a candidate that the rules may merge; the merge of identical inert terms only removes duplicate inert evaluations, as the next call did. Policies: the finishing runs the rules of the policy's own dispatcher; the effect gate decides as on the next call. The comparer is never asked more than the next call asked it, and its own simplifications are not finished.

23.10 Residual limits

  • Factors found by the comparer only. A pair whose shared factor is equal only after the comparer's simplification (raw different) is left to the next call, as before.
  • Large or shared candidates and prefixes. A candidate or a prefix of more than 256 nodes, or a factor of more than 32 nodes that is not the same instance, is not finished: long refused chains keep the historical result, and take a second call as on master.
  • One node per round, two rounds. Each round finishes the deepest candidate. bfsumcoef (different sums with one common coefficient) goes from three calls to two: the finished first call gives 2 * ((x1 + z) + (… + (x7 + z))), whose coefficient, a nested sum built by the successive factorings, is canonicalized by the second call (the seven z merged into 7 * z): a node inside a product, which is not the boundary of the refused chain.
  • Comparer simplifications are not finished (23.3).
  • Special coefficients (NaN, infinities, negative zero) and opaque factors are never finished.
  • Pre-existing. A term that is a multiplicative DAG whose paths multiply makes the boundary tests of M1/M4 walk it (independently of mission B: ExpressionSimplifierBinaryFinishingResourceTests.ExplosiveDags_AreTestedAfterBoundedWork uses distinct large products instead).
  • Free-parameter ties of the canonical order (L-3) are unchanged.
