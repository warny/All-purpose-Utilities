Flatten rule of ExpressionSimplifier (PR B)

Status: first n-ary treatment of the simplifier, restricted to the linear additive chains. Built on the infrastructure of FlattenArchitecture.md (PR A, a9ec71ac). No multiplicative chain, no division, no C3, no change to ExpressionDerivation<T> or ExpressionIntegration<T>.

1. Purpose

Level by level, a sum of n terms costs n - 1 node treatments, and each of them prepares its operands, dispatches the additive rules, runs the factor comparisons of AdditionOfEqualsElements / SubstractionOfEqualsElements (each comparison simplifies both operands again through the comparer) and canonicalizes the whole sub-chain. On a left-associated chain the sub-chain grows by one term per level, and the comparer simplifies a canonical chain that is not a fixed point of the subtraction rules (S5A-12): x0 - x1 - ... - x15 with free parameters is refused by the default budget.

The flatten rule treats a whole chain at once: the terms are prepared once, and the canonicalization runs once.

2. B0 audit: the level by level pipeline

For a binary node e, TransformCore(e) does, in this order:

  1. PrepareExpression(left), then PrepareExpression(right): for the simplifier, FoldNaNOperand(TransformCore(child)). The children are therefore complete results, canonicalized and NaN-folded, before the node sees them.
  2. The node is rebuilt from the prepared children (CopyBinaryExpression).
  3. The candidate rules of the node type run in plan order; the effect gate (AllowsRules) is asked once, when the first candidate matches. The first rule that returns a result ends the treatment: no finalization, no canonicalization.
  4. Otherwise FinalizeExpression: for an ordinary Add/Subtract of a type whose operator cannot throw, CanonicalizeAdditiveExpression(left, right), which collects the terms of both prepared operands (CollectAdditiveTerms, through ordinary Add, Subtract and Negate), pins the terms that are not inert, drops zero constants, aggregates the constants when the level holds at least two of them, sorts (stable), groups, cancels and rebuilds a right-associated chain.
  5. The result goes back to the parent, whose PrepareExpression applies FoldNaNOperand to it.

Consequences that matter for an n-ary treatment:

  • The result of an inner node is what its parent's rules match. A canonical sub-chain is an Add node (or 0 - t, a Subtract); a single term, a constant or a product can also come up when terms cancel.
  • A rule result is not canonical. SubstractionWithAddition returns (a - b) - c as built; the factoring rules return (a + b) * k; AdditionWithZero(t, 0) transforms t again.
  • The sort is stable and free parameters tie (S4: two free parameters of one type have no order), so the order of tied terms is the order in which the level collected them, which depends on the grouping and on the rules that ran below.
  • Constants are aggregated per level, only when a level holds two of them.

Hence the level by level result of a chain is not a function of its signed terms: a + a is 2 * a, a + (b + a) is (a + a) + b; 2*a + 2*b is (a + b) * 2; a - (b + c) is (a - b) - c; (c - b) - a is (0 - b) + ((0 - a) + c) and simplifying that again gives (0 - a) + ((0 - b) + c); ((a + 5) - 5) - 3 is (0 - 3) + a while (a + 5) - 8 is -3 + a.

3. Linear chains

A chain rooted at an ordinary Add or Subtract node is linear when (ExpressionSimplifier.IsLinearChain):

  1. its type is double, float, int, long, uint or ulong, and every crossed node is an ordinary operator (IsOrdinaryBinaryArithmetic); the crossed nodes are the Add and Subtract nodes the default CanFlattenThrough accepts (no operator method, intrinsic non-nullable result type), so decimal, checked, lifted and user-defined operators are operands, and a Negate is an operand too (it is not declared);
  2. no Subtract has a crossed right operand, and no crossed node has two constant operands;
  3. every term is a linear term: a parameter; a finite non-zero constant (a boxed primitive of exactly the chain type); or an ordinary product of a parameter by another parameter, or by a finite constant other than 0, 1 and -1;
  4. no parameter occurs twice in the chain (as a term or as a factor), no constant coefficient value occurs twice, and no coefficient equals a constant term;
  5. the chain holds at most two constant terms.

3.1 Preparing a linear term

A parameter and a constant are their own preparation. A product c * x, x * c or x * y is prepared by the multiplicative rules into a product (Multiplication(left, const) commutes the constant first; the zero and one identities do not match; MultiplicationOfEqualsElements compares two distinct operands and declines; the finalization orders the factors). No user code runs, nothing throws, and the prepared term is never a sum, a difference, a negation or a constant. The NaN fold cannot apply (no non-finite constant).

3.2 No rule applies at any level

By induction on the levels, with the matrix above. The only rule that runs is the factoring rule, and its comparisons fail:

  • two operands of one node never hold the same parameter (condition 4), and the comparer's simplifier never introduces a parameter, so L' and R', or a factor of one and the other operand, cannot simplify to equal trees;
  • a constant coefficient is compared with another coefficient (distinct values), with a parameter, or with a constant term (distinct from every coefficient);
  • the constant 1 the rule supplies for an operand that is not a product is compared with a factor, never 1; an operand that is the constant 1 makes the rule decline before any comparison.

Nothing cancels (no repeated parameter), so a sub-chain with k non-constant terms keeps k groups, and every group holds one term.

3.3 The per-level canonicalizations compose

Every level therefore ends with CanonicalizeAdditiveExpression on its two operands. Its result is rebuilt from one-term groups in sorted order (t or 0 - t), and the parent collects it back in that same order. A stable sort of the concatenation of two stably sorted lists is the stable sort of the concatenation of their sources, so by induction the root orders the terms as one stable sort of the source order does. The key comparer is the same at every level (one lexical scope: a chain never crosses a binder).

Constants: with at most two constant terms, the level where the second one arrives is the only aggregation, with the two constants in source order, exactly as one aggregation of the whole list does. With three or more it differs (found by the differential campaign): a floating-point sum depends on the grouping, and when the first two cancel the third stays alone and is not rewritten (0 - 3 instead of -3).

So the level by level result is CanonicalizeAdditiveTerms(prepared terms in source order), which is what the rule returns. The helper is the body of CanonicalizeAdditiveExpression, extracted without change; both paths share it.

3.4 What is refused, and what is still accepted below it

Everything else is refused before anything is prepared: a repeated parameter (a + a, a*b + a, a shared sub-chain), equal coefficients (2*a + 2*b), a coefficient equal to a constant term (2 + 2*a), a zero, NaN or infinite constant, the coefficients 0, 1, -1, a subtracted group (a - (b + c)), a pair of constants (a + (1 + 2)), a third constant, a negation, a call, a power, a division, a conversion, a nested product, any node with effects, short, ushort and decimal chains.

A refused chain is transformed from its root exactly as before, and the chains below it are offered in turn: in (x0 - x1 - ... - x9) + Sin(y) the root is refused and its left operand is a linear chain of its own. This also applies to the additive nodes the rules rebuild and transform again (a - (-b) → a + b), and to the trees the comparer simplifies.

Limit: the decision is all or nothing per chain root. One term that is not linear, or one repetition, anywhere in the chain sends the root to the level by level treatment, with its historical cost; only the linear sub-chains below the refused node are treated at once. On a left-associated chain whose last term is the offending one, the whole prefix is a linear sub-chain and most of the gain remains; when the offending term comes first, or when the repetition is far from the start, the levels above the repetition pay the historical cost. Example: the S5 family p (100*x0 - x1*3 - 4*x2 - ..., coefficient i + 2) repeats its first coefficient at term 98: up to 97 terms it is accepted (linear cost), from 99 terms the levels above term 98 are treated as on master, whose cost on this family grows too fast for 256 terms to finish in hours (measured: stopped after three hours on the branch; the same tree is not expected to be faster on master). Splitting a refused chain into maximal linear runs is the natural next step and is not part of this PR: it changes which terms are canonicalized together, so it needs its own proof of compatibility.

4. Mechanism

  • [Flatten(ExpressionType.Add, ExpressionType.Subtract)] private Expression? FlattenAdditiveChain(Expression source, FlattenedTerm[] terms): prepares each term once, in source order, with PrepareExpression (transformation, then NaN fold), collects it with CollectAdditiveTerms, and returns CanonicalizeAdditiveTerms. It never declines.
  • FlattenAdmission(root) (new engine-internal hook, see FlattenArchitecture.md §5): the built-in simplifier admits the linear chains only. The test is one iterative walk of the crossed nodes that stops at the first refusal, reads framework operators and leaves by type tests only (an extension node is never read, an operator method is only tested for provenance), counts one structural visit per node, and is linear in the distinct nodes of a shared DAG.
  • The effect gate is not asked for the built-in type: a linear chain only holds parameters, primitive constants and ordinary products under operators that cannot throw, a subset of what ExpressionEffectAnalysis.HasInertOperands proves inert (a test asserts it on every accepted chain of the differential corpus). The gate analyzes the operands recursively, which would bound the depth of an accepted chain by the execution stack.
  • A chain that is not admitted reports RuleRejected with the new reason ExpressionRuleRejection.ChainNotAdmitted (detailed trace) and is not extracted.

4.1 Derived simplifiers

The rule is private. BuildPlan scans GetMethods(Public | NonPublic | Instance) of the concrete type, which does not return the private methods of a base type: the plan of a derived simplifier holds no flatten rule, and its PrepareExpression, FinalizeExpression, own rules and hiding rules see every node as before. A derived simplifier that declares its own [Flatten] rule opts in for itself, and keeps the effect gate as its admission. No public extension point was added. The comparisons a derived simplifier triggers still simplify with the built-in type (as before), which now flattens the linear chains it meets; results are unchanged.

4.2 Policies

The operands of a linear chain are inert: the gate admits every rule on every node of it under every ExpressionSimplifierOptions, no audited rule runs outside the gate, and the NaN fold of a non-default policy keeps the historical decision on inert arithmetic. The rule is therefore policy-independent; AuditedRules, CanDiscard, CanRepeat and CanReorder are untouched.

4.3 The prepared-operand entry

TransformCoreWithPreparedOperands (two calls, both in AdditionWithNegate) still does not go through the flatten path: it runs the rules and the finalization of one rebuilt Subtract node on operands that are already prepared. It is only reached from a node with a Negate operand, which is never part of a linear chain, so no accepted chain depends on it, and its results are those of master. It was left unchanged on purpose: routing it to the flatten path would hand prepared operands to a rule that prepares its terms.

A rule that calls TransformCore on a rebuilt additive node (SubstractionWithNegate, NegateWithSubstraction, AdditionWithZero, the difference of AdditionWithNegate when no form is reused) offers that node as a chain: its operands are prepared results, so it is linear only if they are linear terms or linear sub-chains, and then §3 applies to it as to a source chain (preparing a prepared linear term again is what the level by level path does too).

5. Budgets and depth

Not identical to master, by design: the rule removes work.

Level by level Accepted linear chain of n terms
Transformation visits 2n - 1 node treatments, plus every treatment of the comparer simplifications of the factoring probes 1 + the preparation of each term (n + 1 for parameters and constants)
Structural visits keys, comparisons, hashes and effect analyses of every level, and of every comparer simplification admission walk (n - 1), extraction (2n - 1), then the keys, sort and grouping of one canonicalization
Execution stack one recursion per level, and the recursive effect analysis constant
  • Every piece of work is still counted, in the session of the operation; a refusal is never absorbed; the instance is reusable after a refusal (security tests, just above each limit).
  • MaxDepth and MaxNodes are validated before any rule, as before: a chain deeper than 512 nodes is refused by the default limits although the rule would treat it without recursion.
  • Refusals that became acceptances. A chain whose level by level work exceeded a budget and is linear is now accepted (x0 - ... - x15, free parameters: 1,960,419 visits on master, 17 now). The reverse does not occur on an accepted chain. A chain that is not linear keeps its level by level counters, minus the work saved on the linear chains below it and in its comparer simplifications.
  • The statement of SimplificationPolicies.md that the default policy keeps the historical resource consumption holds for the policy mechanism; it does not describe this rule, which lowers the consumption of the linear chains under every policy.

6. Compatibility

Under every policy, for the built-in type: same result trees (structure, types, constants, parameter instances) for the first and the second pass, same user-code calls in the same order, same exceptions, as master a9ec71ac, except the resource refusals that became acceptances (§5). Derived simplifiers: unchanged, counters included, except for the work of the comparisons they trigger. Traces of the built-in type differ on the chains concerned (one treatment per chain, the flatten rule as the applied rule, ChainNotAdmitted rejections at the detailed level).

7. Validation

Reference: master a9ec71ac in a separate worktree, same machine, runtime and configuration.

  • Differential corpus (a dedicated harness, the same deterministic corpus simplified by both checkouts, compared line by line): every tree of two terms over a pool of twenty linear and non-linear terms for double, float, int, long, uint and decimal, every tree of three terms and every assignment of +/- for double and int (96,768 trees), and seeded random trees of two to twelve terms, left, right, random and mixed groupings, free, lambda-bound and wrapped under another node, with linear terms, repeated parameters, zero/one/NaN/infinite constants, negations, calls, powers, divisions, checked nodes, unknown calls and logged custom constants. Compared: the result of the first and of the second pass, the exception, the number and the ordered log of the user Equals/GetHashCode calls, the number of effectful calls. 460,008 cases under the default policy: 0 difference. 109,008 cases under each of four other policies (trust, aggressive, no exception preserved, permissive) and with a derived simplifier: 0 difference. The first run of this corpus found the three-constant divergence of §3.3 (4 cases out of 154,008), which narrowed condition 5.
  • S5 campaigns (tests/ExpressionSimplifierAudit/run-audit.ps1, 1,000 seeds, 1,000 nested compositions, default policy): canon, comparer, diff, effects and s3 are byte-identical. converge (64,420 cases): the first pass, the last pass, the category and the sizes are identical; only the list of rule names applied by the second pass differs (FlattenAdditiveChain appears in 8,419 cases). terminate (63,270 cases): classes, exceptions, results and output sizes are identical; the counters differ: 739,669 treatments on master, 618,329 on the branch (−16.4 %), fewer in 12,719 cases, more in 2 cases (at most +7; not investigated further), trace depth lower in 4,349 cases and never higher.
  • Suites: UtilsTest.Unit, UtilsTest.Security and UtilsTest.Functional pass.
  • Mutations (applied one at a time, then removed): a term prepared twice, the terms in reverse order, the signs ignored, the admission always granted, a call accepted as a term, repeated parameters, a third constant, a subtracted group, zero and non-finite constants, equal coefficients, a pair of constants, the gate of derived simplifiers removed: each makes at least one test of ExpressionSimplifierFlattenTests fail. One mutation survives because it is equivalent: preparing the terms with TransformCore instead of PrepareExpression (no linear term can hold a NaN operation). The mutations of the brief about decimal and about the prepared-operand entry have nothing to mutate: decimal chains are not crossed, and that entry is not on the flatten path.

Tests whose input was a linear chain used as a vehicle were given another vehicle, with the same assertions (see the PR description): two tracing tests, one comparer diagnostic test, and the budget tests of UtilsTest.Security that relied on the S5 families g and on a left sum of 100 parameters to exhaust a budget. S5A12 and S5A08 of the deep-audit tests now pin the level by level growth on a derived simplifier, respectively a lower budget, and the new behavior of the built-in type.

Acceptance (differential corpus, default policy, both passes and the comparer simplifications included): every chain offered to the rule is accepted, since the admission runs before the offer. 257,220 chains were simplified at once; 55.2 % of the 118,008 cases contain at least one (double 48.3 %, int 67.3 %, long 86.1 %, float 80.9 %, uint 86.0 %, decimal 0 %, the type is not crossed).

Residual risks.

  • Admission is all or nothing per root: a long chain with one non-linear term (the p family from term 98) keeps the level by level cost, even on its linear part. Its linear sub-chains below the offending term are still accepted.
  • The admission walk is repeated for every sub-chain of a refused chain (allocations above).
  • A future change of a rule or of the canonical order must keep the proof of §3 valid; the differential corpus and the exhaustive grouping test are the guard.
