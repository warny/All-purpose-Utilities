# Utils.NumberToString audit closures — 2026-10-07

NTS-15 and NTS-18 are closed, which completes the Italian ordinals. "Closed" does not mean that every
`long` now produces a string: every Italian ordinal category now has a deliberate contract —
**supported** when the form is sourced or follows a productive rule established by the sources,
**unsupported** (`NotSupportedException`) when no canonical composition or spelling could be
established. Sources, negative searches and the three levels of evidence are recorded in
`docs/NTS-08-linguistic-sources.md` ("Italian closure — NTS-15 and NTS-18").

No engine code changed: `<OrdinalStem>`, ordinal-only `<Replacement>`, `<OrdinalScale>` and
`<OrdinalComposition>` cover the whole sourced domain. `OrdinalScaleRule`, `OrdinalCompositionRule`,
`NumberToStringConverter*.cs` and the XSD are untouched.

## Round biliardo and trilione ordinals (NTS-18)

- **Sources**: *trilionesimo* is attested as an ordinal (Nuovo De Mauro, GDLI: agg. num. ord.).
  *biliardesimo* is lexically attested — ADN 2017, vol. I, Italian translation by the Ministero
  delle Infrastrutture e dei Trasporti, §1.2.2.1, table of decimal multiples and submultiples
  (*Biliardesimo* 10^-15, *Trilionesimo* 10^-18), read in the PDF — but that table gives the
  fractional (submultiple) reading, not the ordinal one; the ordinal use of *biliardesimo* is
  licensed by the general Treccani rule (cardinal stem + *-esimo*, as *miliardo* → *miliardesimo*).
  Together they invalidate the former statement that these scale ordinals were unsourced.
- **Configuration**: `<OrdinalScale scales="2..6" multiplierSeparator="" />` (was `2..4`). The scale
  names of indices 5 and 6 were already *biliardo* and *trilione* (NTS-14).
- **Productivity, not attestation**: the forms with a multiplier above one (*duebiliardesimo*,
  *duetrilionesimo*, *novecentonovantanovebiliardesimo*, *novetrilionesimo*) apply the same rule
  already retained for *duemillesimo*, *duemilionesimo*, *duemiliardesimo* and *duebilionesimo*;
  they are not individually attested.
- **Junctions checked by test, not guessed**: the existing *trébili* replacement already covers
  biliardo (*ventitrebiliardesimo*); a trilione multiplier is at most 9 in the `long` domain, so
  no *trétri* junction can arise and no replacement was added (*tretrilionesimo*); compounds of
  *uno* keep the plural-noun form (*ventunobiliardesimo*).

## Domain guard

`ItalianOrdinalLanguageSpecifics` no longer rejects everything from a biliardo. From a million it
takes the largest unit of a static table (10^6, 10^9, 10^12, 10^15, 10^18) not above the value and
rejects the value unless it is a multiple of that unit. The scan only compares, so it cannot
overflow; 10^15, 2 × 10^15, 999 × 10^15, 10^18 and 9 × 10^18 are accepted, 10^15 + 1, 10^18 + 1,
9 × 10^18 + 1 and `long.MaxValue` rejected. The plugin still never builds a form.

## Deliberate limitations (NTS-15, rest of NTS-18)

A targeted search (see the sources document) found no normative or academic statement that the
juxtaposition ordinal(head) + ordinal(tail) is productive beyond *millesimo* and *centomillesimo*
(the only heads explicitly exemplified in the consulted Treccani material — which does not mean
Treccani excludes others),
no attested *duemillesimo primo* / *duemillesimoprimo*, *milionesimo primo* / *milionesimoprimo*,
and no rule selecting the split of 1110–1910 (*millesimo centodecimo* or *millecentesimo decimo*).
The only proposal for 1 000 001 (Italian Language Stack Exchange) is self-declared virtual. The
following therefore stay rejected, permanently, without an open ticket:

- 1110, 1210 … 1910;
- the non-round thousands above 1999 outside 100001–100009;
- the non-round values from a million.

The engine could compose them; choosing one unsourced candidate would be arbitrary. All historical
decisions are unchanged: 1001–1999 synthetic forms (NTS-13), 1010 *millesimo decimo*, 100001–100009
*centomillesimoprimo* …

## Verification

Test-first: 22 new or changed Italian tests (feature scenarios for biliardo/trilione in both genders
and the new rejection boundaries, guard boundary data rows, the round-scale sweep extended to scales
5–6 with every multiplier that fits a `long`) ran red before the configuration. A before/after dump
of the 73 built-in cultures (cardinals and ordinals 0–2100, scale samples up to `long.MaxValue`,
every variant value, clock times) differs only by 30 lines: the IT round biliardo/trilione ordinals
in the default, maschile and femminile variants (previously `NotSupportedException`). No cardinal
and no other language changed.
