## A — Czech compound ending in *jedna*

**Sources.** IJP "Členění čísel" (#1): every part of a multi-word numeral may be declined, the strategy the plugin
already uses (*před třemi sty šedesáti pěti lety*). IJP "Počítaný předmět" (#2): after a compound ending in one, the
noun agrees with the last member (*dvacet jeden žák*) or stands in the genitive (*dvacet jedna žáků*). Corpus examples
(Žďárek 2009, #3) attest the agreement with the declined final *jeden* in the oblique cases: *dožil se devadesáti
jednoho roku*, *po sto jednom roce*, *sto padesát jedním hlasem*.

**Strategy.** The final *jedna* is declined with the rest and agrees with the feminine fraction noun, which stands in
the singular of the case. The nominative and accusative keep the counting form and the genitive plural (*dvacet jedna
třetin*; #2, the more usual construction). Level: rule + attested construction; the combination with a fraction noun is
an application (no source quotes *dvaceti jedné třetiny*). No list of exceptions: `EndsInCompoundOne` (n % 10 = 1,
n % 100 ≠ 11, n ≥ 2) covers units after the tens (21), after the hundreds (101), after a scale group (1001, 1 000 001)
and after the generated scales (10^12 + 1).

## B — Czech multiplier ending in one

**Rule shared with A.** A scale noun (*tisíc*, *milion*, *miliarda*, *bilion* …) is a noun counted by its multiplier;
under the all-parts strategy (#1) the multiplier's final one agrees with it exactly as a numerator's final one agrees with
the fraction noun. Both go through the same private helper, `FinalOne(case, gender)`, called from `AppendDeclinedGroup`
(a one after the tens or hundreds) and `DeclineCardinal` (a one after a larger group); `AppendDeclinedScale` passes the
scale noun's gender and puts the noun in the singular of the case after a multiplier ending in a compound one. The
`finalOneInstrumental` flag is gone: the *lomeno* *jednou* is `FinalOne(Instrumental, Feminine)`.

**Genders.** *tisíc*, *milion*, *bilion*, *trilion* … masculine (*jednomu tisíci*, *jedním milionem*); *miliarda*,
*biliarda* … feminine (*jedné miliardě*, *jednou miliardou*), as NTS-30B/42 F already declare them.

**The inverted form.** The IJP also allows *jednadvaceti* (*před jednadvaceti tisíci diváky*); it is not the
configuration's word order and has no form for 101, so the output keeps the non-inverted order.

## D — *lomeno*/*lomené* under an external case (kept closed)

**Search.** IJP and the ÚJČ poradna database (its answers load by script and could not be queried), Naše řeč
(Prandstetter 1938), Slovenská reč (Peciar 1958, Ondrus 1968, Peciar 1976, the 1978 note citing Ondrus), Kultúra slova (2002/5 and the
1986–2019 issues at hand), school and university mathematics pages in both languages.

**Result.** Every source reads the slash phrase alone: CS *X lomeno Y(instr.)* (#8), SK *X lomené Y(instr.)* (#7, #9:
*jedna lomené troma*, *jeden lomené sto štyridsiatimi dvoma*). No sentence was found where the whole phrase is governed
by a preposition or a case.

**Open question, exactly.** When a fraction read with the slash operator is itself governed by an external case, does
the first operand X take that case (*s dvěma lomeno třemi*, *s dvoma lomené troma*) or stay the nominative of the
arithmetic term (*s dvě lomeno třemi*, *s dva lomené troma*)? The ordinary declension of the cardinals is not evidence
for an operator phrase, so the reading stays fail-closed (also for a zero over a denominator without a fraction noun).

## E/SK — Slovak zero (opened)

**Sources.** Š. Peciar, "Nula celých, či nula celá?", Slovenská reč 23 (1958), 117–118 (#5): *nula* is indeclinable with
a counted noun, "dobre vidno z nepriamych pádov": *ochladiť na nula stupňov* (not *na nulu*), *z nula percent* (not *z
nuly*), *s nula celými* (not *s nulou*), like *sto* and *tisíc* whose noun alone changes. Kultúra slova 36 (2002), 5
(#6): the noun *nula* declines (*napísať nulu*, *nad nulou*), the quantifier does not (*pri nula stupňoch*). Peciar 1976
(#7) applies it to decimals (*nula celých päť desatín*).

**Rule.** *nula* stays, the fraction noun takes the case; the accusative keeps the genitive plural of the nominative
(*na nula stupňov*). The paradigms are those the plugin already holds (*žena*, *ulica*).

| Fraction | Gen. | Dat. | Acc. | Loc. | Instr. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0/3 | nula tretín | nula tretinám | nula tretín | nula tretinách | nula tretinami |
| 0/2 | nula polovíc | nula poloviciam | nula polovíc | nula poloviciach | nula polovicami |
| 0/100, 0/1000, 0/10^6 | nula stotín … | nula stotinám | … | nula milióntinách | nula tisícinami |

Before: `NotSupportedException` in every oblique case. The caller's gender changes nothing; a zero over a denominator
without a noun (0/23) stays closed with D.

## E/CS — Czech zero (kept closed)

**Level of evidence: usage attested, not normative.** Usage has an indeclinable *nula* (*při nula stupních*). The IJP,
ASCS, SSČ and SSJČ (#4) decline *nula* as a feminine noun (*nuly, nule, nulou*) and quote it as a quantifier in the
nominative only (*nula celá pět desetin*; #2: *nula/žádná celá dva metrů*). No Czech source states the Slovak rule, and
the lexical *nulou* is precisely the analysis Peciar rejects for Slovak: neither reading is opened.
