# NTS-42 — audit of the UK, CS and SK fraction closures left by NTS-40/41 (2026-10-19)

Opened by NTS-40/41 (`DONE-2026-10-16(1).md`). Starting master: `4cd45b72` (NTS-43, #647). Audit-first: each of the six
families was reproduced separately, located in the pipeline, and qualified. **No production code changed**: no engine,
plugin, XML, XSD or public API change; the audit adds tests that pin every closure and its open neighbours.

## Where the readings are built

```text
ConvertFraction(n, d, variants)
  → BuildFractionText                       (generic engine, NumberToStringConverter)
      1. named power-of-ten <Fractions>     (not configured for UK, CS, SK)
      2. IFractionLanguageSpecifics.TryGetFractionDenominator(n, d, variants)
           false → NotSupportedException    (the only closure path, no language test in the engine)
           reading.NumeratorText → ApplyRawAdjustment(text) + " " + reading.Text   (NTS-40 oblique numerators)
           otherwise → BuildCardinalFragment(n, forced ⊕ variants) + " " + reading.Text
  → FinalizePhrase (sign, finalization once)
```

The engine has no special path for zero, a case, a slash reading or a scale: zero and the caller's case reach the
plugin like any other numerator (test `E_ZeroReachesThePluginWithTheCase`). Every NTS-42 family is decided inside the
language plugin classes — `CzechOrdinalLanguageSpecifics`, `SlovakOrdinalLanguageSpecifics`,
`UkrainianOrdinalLanguageSpecifics` and their `*.FractionCases.cs` parts. These are public classes of this repository
(not external private plugins); their declined-cardinal builders are `private` to each class, as NTS-40/41 designed them.

## Decisions

The first version of this audit qualified A–E as "contract insufficient", which mixed three different situations. The
revision below (same day, after an independent source search) separates them:

- **no exact source**: no source reads the construction itself (a fraction in that case);
- **no general rule**: no source gives a rule from which the construction follows without a choice;
- **no mechanism**: the engine or the plugin contract cannot represent the reading.

For A–E the third one never applies: `FractionDenominatorReading` carries a plugin-spelled numerator (`NumeratorText`,
NTS-40) and any denominator text, so every candidate reading listed below is representable by the CS, SK and UK plugins
as they are. The question is only linguistic. F is the only family with a missing mechanism.

| family | reproduced? | responsible layer | level of evidence | decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A — CS *jedna* in a compound | yes, deliberate closure | CS plugin (`DeclineCardinal`/`AppendDeclinedGroup`) | **partially documented** — rules and corpus examples for the agreement types; no source chooses one for an oblique compound ending in *jedna*, and none reads it with a fraction noun | kept closed |
| B — CS multiplier ending in one | yes, deliberate closure | CS plugin (`AppendDeclinedScale`) | **partially documented** — IJP rules for multi-word numerals and the scale nouns; the nominative of the multiplier itself varies (*dvacet jedna / dvacet jeden tisíc*); no oblique example | kept closed |
| C — SK bare *jeden* | yes, deliberate closure | SK plugin (`DeclineLastComponent`) | **documented (rule + construction type)** — *stojeden / sto jeden* and the higher compounds ending in *jeden* are indeclinable like *dvadsaťjeden*; the noun alone takes the case (*so stojeden družstvami*). The fraction-noun combination is the same inference the plugin already makes for 21 and 121 | kept closed in this PR; opening moved to **NTS-44** |
| D — *lomeno*/*lomené* oblique | yes, deliberate closure | CS/SK plugins (`TryGetFractionDenominator`) | **not established** — *lomeno/lomené* is an invariable word governing the instrumental (documented); the behaviour of the whole *x lomeno y* phrase under a governing case has no source and at least two plausible readings | kept closed |
| E — zero oblique | yes, deliberate closure | UK/CS/SK plugins | **lexical declension documented, combination not established** — *нуль*, *nula* (CS, SK) decline as nouns; no source reads a declined zero with a fraction noun or a *lomeno* phrase; UK has no zero fraction even in the nominative | kept closed |
| F — ≥ 10^12 | yes, deliberate bound | CS/SK/UK builders (`MaxDeclinedNumber`, `MaxDeclinedNumerator`) | not a linguistic question — **no mechanism** | **deferred / dependency**: see below |

None of A–E has a defect in the generic engine. No converter behaviour changes in this PR: C is the only family whose
evidence could justify opening it, and doing so is a behaviour change to review on its own (exact outputs below).

### A — CS compound numerator ending in *jedna*

- **Rule** (S2): in the nominative after a compound ending in *jeden … čtyři* both *dvacet jeden žák* and *dvacet jedna
  žáků* are possible, the genitive "běžnější a přirozenější". The plugin's nominative follows the second one (*dvacet
  jedna třetin*).
- **Rule** (S1): three strategies for a multi-word numeral in an oblique case — every part declined (*bez tisíce osmi set
  čtyřiceti sedmi korun*), only the tens and units (*bez tisíc osm set čtyřiceti sedmi korun*), or none, "většinou v
  mluvené řeči" (*k tisíc sedm set dvacet dva korunám*). None of the S1 examples ends in *jedna*.
- **Attested** (S4, corpus examples, oblique cases, singular agreement only): *dožil se devadesáti jednoho roku*, *po sto
  jednom roce*, *sto padesát jedním hlasem*. S4 also states that "mluvnice a příručky neposkytují žádné vodítko" for the
  choice of agreement and that in the oblique cases the answers show "velká variabilita".
- **Rule** (S3): the inverted *jednadvacet* (also *jedenadvacet*) declines as a whole (2., 3., 6., 7. *jednadvaceti*) and
  counts with the genitive plural (S4: *jednadvacet lingvistů*); S3 notes that *dvacet jedna* is the established
  form when naming the number. The inverted form exists only for 21–99 (S4: not for 101).
- **Deductions, not attested for a fraction**, each consistent with one strategy:
  1. singular agreement declined — *dvaceti jedné třetině* (dative), *dvaceti jednou třetinou*; it reverses the
     nominative choice (*dvacet jedna třetina* would be its nominative);
  2. inverted form — *jednadvaceti třetinám*; changes the word order of the configuration and does not reach 101, 1001;
  3. undeclined numeral, noun in the case — *dvacet jedna třetinám* (S1 third strategy, marked as mostly spoken).
- **Contract**: all three are representable (`NumeratorText`). **Status: partially documented** — the choice between
  three sourced strategies is a decision no source makes for this construction. For comparison, the CS slash
  denominator already reads a compound-final *jedna* as the feminine *jednou* (*jedna lomeno dvaceti jednou*): there the
  number is a bare arithmetic term (NTS-41, inference from S6), not a counted quantity, so that choice does not carry
  over to a numerator that agrees with a fraction noun.

### B — CS multiplier ending in one

- **Rule** (S2): after *sto, tisíc, milion, miliarda* the genitive is the basic construction, case agreement possible
  (*s třemi tisíci diváků/diváky*). **Rule** (S1): the multi-word strategies above apply to the multiplier.
- **Attested** (S4): in the nominative "můžeme říci *dvacet jedna tisíc* i *dvacet jeden tisíc*", the second being more
  frequent in the corpus; one corpus example *tisíc jeden milion korun*. No oblique example.
- **Deductions**: *dvaceti jednomu tisíci třetin* (singular agreement with the masculine *tisíc*, dative) or *dvaceti
  jedna tisícům třetin* (undeclined unit, plural scale noun) — the gender and number of *tisíc* after a compound-final
  *jeden/jedna* outside the nominative are not given anywhere; *miliarda* (feminine) would follow another agreement
  again.
- **Contract**: representable. **Status: partially documented**, less than A: the variation already exists in the
  nominative, which the plugin pins to *dvacet jedna tisíc*.

### C — SK numerator ending in a bare *jeden*

- **Rule** (S8, 326): the compounds *dvadsaťjeden, tridsaťjeden …* are indeclinable, the counted noun alone declines as
  after *päť*; **attested**: *štyridsaťjeden družstiev, zo štyridsaťjeden družstiev, ku štyridsaťjeden družstvám, v
  štyridsaťjeden družstvách, so štyridsaťjeden družstvami*. S8 327: *sto* (also in compounds) stays undeclined before a
  noun (*k sto robotníkom, so sto robotníkmi*); 328–329: the compounds with *tisíc* are usually undeclined.
- **Rule** (S7, 223, table 2.4.1): "*101 stojeden / sto jeden* – nesklonné, bez kongruenčnej kategórie mužskej osoby";
  (S7, 226, conclusion): "vyššie zložené číslovky končiace sa na číslovku jeden (podobne ako typ dvadsaťjeden) sú
  nesklonné a nemajú kategóriu mužskej osoby". Footnote 17 (p. 221): the *-jeden* compounds are indeclinable.
- **Attested** (S7, 211, quoting PSP 1953, 88–89): "nesklonnosť zložených čísloviek obsahujúcich v druhej časti
  zloženiny číslovku jeden: *dvadsaťjeden, stojeden, tisícjeden vojakov, žien; k dvadsaťjeden vojakom, ženám; so
  stojeden družstvami*". This is the exact construction type (indeclinable numeral ending in *jeden* + noun in the case),
  from a superseded codification; S7 restates the indeclinability as current.
- **Spelling**: S7 admits both *stojeden* and *sto jeden* for the higher compounds; the plugin writes *sto jeden*.
- **Deduction** (same as the open 21 and 121): *sto jeden tretín* (genitive), *sto jeden tretinám*, *o sto jeden
  tretinách*, *sto jeden tretinami*; *tisíc jeden …* likewise. The bare *jeden* after *milión/miliarda* (10^6 + 1,
  10^9 + 1) falls under S7's general statement ("vyššie zložené číslovky končiace sa na číslovku jeden") without an
  example.
- **Contract**: representable without any new notion (the indeclinable path used for 21). **Status: documented (rule +
  construction type)**; the fraction-noun combination is a deduction of the same level as the readings already shipped.
  NTS-08 #10 already cited S7 for *stojeden* indeclinable; the first audit overlooked it. Not opened here: it would
  change outputs of the dump (101, 1001, 2001, 10^6 + 1, 10^9 + 1 in four oblique cases), which this audit-only PR does
  not do.

### D — *lomeno*/*lomené* under an oblique case

- **Attested** (S6): *dvě lomeno třemi* (SSJČ), *pět lomeno dvěma* (SSČ); the rule "jmenovatele čteme v sedmém pádu".
  *lomeno* is invariable; it is not *lomeno* that would have to decline.
- **Attested pattern** (S6, Prandstetter, as recorded in NTS-08 #6): in the instrumental expression *čtyřmi iks minus
  jedna* only the first term is declined, the term after the operator stays in the nominative. (Not re-read in this
  revision: the archive server refused the connection.)
- S5 describes *lomeno* as an operator (and as a coordinator/adordinator) — read from the abstract only; it is not known
  whether it discusses case.
- **SK**: Ondrus 1968 (NTS-08 #14) gives *jedna lomené dvoma*, *jedna lomené číslom dvadsaťjeden*; no oblique use.
- **Deductions** for a dative context, none attested:
  1. only the first term takes the governing case, as in the S6 pattern — *dvěma lomeno třemi*;
  2. the whole expression stays a quoted arithmetic term, as in S1's third strategy — *dvě lomeno třemi*;
  3. a noun takes the case and the expression stays nominative (*číslu dvě lomeno třemi*) — a rewording, not a reading.
- **Contract**: 1 and 2 are representable (`NumeratorText` + "lomeno …"); 3 is outside the fraction reading.
  **Status: not established**.

### E — zero numerator under an oblique case

- **Attested lexical declension**: UK *нуль, нуля, нулеві/нулю, нуль, нулем, нулю/нулі/нулеві* (S10); CS *nula, nuly,
  nule, nulu, nule, nulou* (S3); SK *nula, nuly, nule, nulu, nule, nulou* (S9).
- **Rule** (S8, 317): in the positions of the nominative, genitive and accusative a noun counted by *nula* is in the
  genitive plural (*nula stupňov*, *nula celých*); "v ostatných pádoch podstatné meno je v príslušnom páde" is stated
  for the numerals in general, and the form *nula* itself takes there is not given.
- **Attested in decimals only**: CS *nula celá* (NTS-08), SK *nula celých* (S8, S9), UK *нуль цілих*.
- **Deductions**: SK *k nule tretín* / *k nula tretinám*; CS *nule lomeno třemi* / *nula lomeno třemi* (D applies);
  UK — no nominative zero fraction exists to decline (*нуль третіх* is itself unsourced, NTS-39).
- **Contract**: representable. **Status: lexical declension documented; combination not established**.

### F — the 10^12 bound

- The builders spell and decline *tisíc*, *milion*, *miliarda* (and UK *тисяча*, *мільйон*, *мільярд*) from their own
  paradigm tables and stop at `999 999 999 999`. From 10^12 the names are **generated** by the engine's single
  Conway-Guy-Wechsler machinery (`NumberScale` + `<GeneratedScaleForm>`, NTS-30C), which only provides the count-agreement
  forms of the nominative (*dva biliony*, *pět bilionů*, *dvě biliardy*). A plugin has no access to these generated names
  or to their oblique forms.
- Opening F would need either a second spelling of the generated names inside the plugins (a duplicate of the Conway
  machinery — excluded) or a **generic** way for the engine to supply a generated scale noun in a requested case to a
  fraction plugin (a new engine/contract capability, possibly XML case forms on `<GeneratedScaleForm>`): a design
  decision outside NTS-42, to be specified before any code.
- From 10^24 the CS/SK names also depend on **NTS-36** (*quadrilion* → *kvadrilion*): declining them before NTS-36 would
  decline the wrong spelling.
- The current behaviour is the right fail-closed one: a deterministic `NotSupportedException` at an explicit bound, the
  nominative numerators reading the generated cardinal unchanged (`F_NominativeNumerators_UseTheGeneratedScales`).
