# NTS-30E — Finnish cardinal composition and thousand agreement (2026-10-14)

Split out of NTS-30, after NTS-30D. Starting master: `ae0758d0dec059cbb7ba2e6267dd2fca97caae90`.
Sources: `docs/NTS-08-linguistic-sources.md`, section NTS-30E.

## Root cause

Finnish was composed like a spaced language: the tens and hundreds `buildString` carried a space
(`kaksikymmentä *`, `sata *`), the multiplier and the scale noun were joined by the language separator (a space),
and the thousands had a single form *tuhat*: `kaksikymmentä yksi`, `sata yksi`, `kaksi tuhat`. The case variants were
written for that spacing (`LastWord` units, `Standalone` *sata*/*tuhat*) and left the multiplier in the nominative
(partitive `kaksikymmentä yhtä`, genitive `sata yhden`).

## Linguistic rule (Kielitoimiston ohjepankki)

- The part below a thousand is one word, so is the part counting the thousands; groups are separated by a space
  (*neljätoistatuhatta kuusisataa*, *viisimiljoonaa neljäsataakaksikymmentäkuusituhatta
  kuusisataaseitsemänkymmentäneljä*). *tuhat* after the elided one (*tuhat viisisataa*), *tuhatta* otherwise.
- *miljoona*, *miljardi* … are usually written apart from their multiplier (*kolme miljoonaa*): the junction
  multiplier ↔ scale noun differs per scale.
- Inflected cardinals inflect every component, in one word (*kahtakymmentäyhtä*, *sadankahdenkymmenenviiden*,
  *kolmeatuhatta*); *-toista* never inflects.

## Generic primitive

Three boundaries, three settings:

| Setting | Boundary |
|---|---|
| `Language separator` | words composed through the language (connectors, decimals, fractions, currencies, digit-by-digit); historical multiplier ↔ scale noun |
| `Language groupSeparator` (+ `separator`) | between two scale groups |
| `NumberScale multiplierSeparator` | multiplier ↔ scale noun, every scale, generated ones included |
| `ScaleForm multiplierSeparator` | multiplier ↔ scale noun, one exact scale, whichever form is selected |

- Resolution: exact `<ScaleForm multiplierSeparator>` → `NumberScale multiplierSeparator` → language `separator`.
  No generated-scale override (no use case).
- `scaleConnector`, when its threshold is reached, keeps `separator + connector + separator` unchanged.
- Presence-sensitive strings: absent = inherit / fall back, `""` = joined, never trimmed. `baseOn`: absent inherits,
  explicit (empty included) replaces; a child `<ScaleForm scale="n">` replaces the inherited entry whole.
- Programmatic: `NumberScale.MultiplierSeparator` (`init`, like `SuffixSeparator`);
  `NumberToStringConverterOptions.ScaleFormMultiplierSeparators` (`IReadOnlyDictionary<int, string>`, parallel to
  `ScaleFormSelectors`: the exact scale forms are already modelled as per-index dictionaries, and `LexicalFormSet` is
  shared with the time units, so it cannot carry a scale-only junction); converter snapshot
  `NumberToStringConverter.ScaleFormMultiplierSeparators`, copied by the options clone. Keys must be nameable scale
  indices ≥ 1 and values non-null (`ArgumentException`).
- **Explicit built-in defaults (review of #640).** The engine fallback stays the language separator (compatibility of
  programmatic and custom XML configurations), but the built-in bases now state their convention:
  `multiplierSeparator=" "` on `SCALE-SHORT`, `SCALE-LONG` and `SCALE-SHORT-CYRILLIC`. The historical junction was the
  language separator, not a space, so the dump identified the languages this changes: only HU/HU-HU, which inherits
  `SCALE-LONG` with `separator=""` (3 558 lines: `ezer` → `egy ezer`, `kétezer` → `kettő ezer`). HU therefore declares
  `multiplierSeparator=""`; with it the dump is again FI-only (identical report).

## Finnish configuration

- `buildString` without spaces; `separator=" "` and `groupSeparator=""` kept (the separator also spaces decimals,
  fractions and currencies, so emptying it would glue `kaksikymmentäyksipilkkuviisi`).
- `<NumberScale multiplierSeparator=" ">` and `<ScaleForm scale="1" multiplierSeparator="">` with *tuhat* / *tuhatta*
  under the default selector.
- The elision of one stays a scale-1 replacement on the whole group, now `yksituhat → tuhat` (as Hungarian
  `egyezer → ezer`); the selector already yields *tuhat* for one.
- Partitive and genitive: ordered `Anywhere` replacements of every component; *sataa*, *tuhatta*, *miljoonaa* are
  first brought back to their bare form so nothing is inflected twice.
- Domain unchanged (`maxNumber="999999"`); the `miljoona` suffix and variant rules stay dormant.

## Regression dump

All 74 converters against `ae0758d0`, 1 693 712 lines: **2 383 changed, all FI** (cardinals, negatives, decimals in
every reading mode, fractions, `Number`, currencies, years, variant cardinals). Ordinals (`O`), clock times (`K`) and
times (`T`) are byte-identical; no other culture changes.
