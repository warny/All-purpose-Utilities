# Utils.NumberToString audit closures — 2026-10-07 (1)

NTS-16 (Wolof and Ewe ordinal formation unsourced) is closed. The audit found two different
situations: the Wolof ordinal is corrected on sourced evidence, while the Ewe ordinal cannot be
corrected without rebuilding the Ewe cardinals, which is now a separate ticket (NTS-20). A third
finding, the Wolof cardinals from a thousand, is tracked by NTS-19. Sources and decisions are
recorded in `docs/NTS-08-linguistic-sources.md` ("NTS-16 — Wolof and Ewe ordinals").

No engine primitive changed: `NumberToStringConverter*.cs`, the XSD, `<OrdinalStem>`,
`<OrdinalComposition>` and `<OrdinalScale>` are untouched. The only new code is
`WolofOrdinalLanguageSpecifics`, a per-language domain guard of the same kind as
`ItalianOrdinalLanguageSpecifics` (it builds no text).

## Wolof — corrected

- **Old**: `-ël` (`ñaarël`, `fukkël`), unattested. **New**: `-eel` on the last element of the
  cardinal — the existing suffix pipeline, configuration `<Ordinals suffix="eel">`.
- **Sources**: Kosogorova, *Studies in African Languages and Cultures* 57 (2023), §4.2 (rule and
  `juróom ñetteel`, `fukk(a) ak ñaareel`, `ñaar fukk(a) ak ñenteel`; Ngom 2003: `ñaar-eel-u xarit
  bi`, `fukk-eel-u fas wi`, `ñett-eel-u rééw mi`); Robert, "Wolof: A grammatical sketch" (LLACAN,
  CNRS & INALCO, preprint 2021 of the Oxford guide to the Atlantic languages): "ordinal numbers are
  obtained by suffixation of an –eel morpheme to the cardinal number (e.g. juróóm ñaar-eel
  'seventh')". Both read in full text.
- **Spelling `-eel`, not `-éél`**: both academic authors mark the closed long vowel with acute
  accents (`tééméér`, `juróóm`) yet write the suffix unaccented, i.e. the open vowel; decree
  2005-992 spells the open long vowel `ee` and puts a single accent on a closed long vowel
  (`ée`, as the configuration's `téeméer`). Janga Wolof's `-éél` matches neither the vowel quality
  nor the decree.
- **First**: `bu njëkk` is kept. It is the class-B relative form of the verb `jëkk` "to be first"
  (Robert; Kosogorova after Ngom 2003: `xale b-u njëkk`), cited as the standalone list form by
  Janga Wolof (`bu njëk`) and close to Omniglot (`jëk, njëk`); `njëkk` follows the geminate spelling
  of `jëkk`.
- **Domain**: 1–999. The rule is stated generally ("the last element of a cardinal numeral", the
  only exception being 'one'), so the values not individually exemplified (`fukk ak benneel`,
  `téeméereel`, `ñaar téeméer ak ñett-fukk ak ñenteel`) are a productive application, not
  attestations. Kosogorova does not say that ordinals become rare above the tens; she notes a
  general scarcity of data on Wolof ordinals.
- **From 1000: rejected** by `WolofOrdinalLanguageSpecifics` (with zero, as before). The configured
  thousands cardinal diverges from both academic sources (no `ak` after `junni`: `benn junni benn`
  vs `juuni ak juróom ñaar`; `benn junni` vs `junni` for 1000), and `junni` + `-eel` (vowel-final)
  is exemplified only by Omniglot (`junneel`). Opened NTS-19.
- **Hyphens**: the configuration writes `juróom-ñett`, `ñaar-fukk` where the sources write a space;
  the ordinal follows the configured cardinal (`juróom-ñetteel`). Part of NTS-19.

## Ewe — ordinals withdrawn, cardinals to rebuild

- **Old**: `etsõ` + cardinal, the exception receiving the prefix too (`etsõ gbãtõ`, `etsõ eve`),
  unattested. **Source formation**: cardinal + `-lia`, first = `gbãtɔ` — Ewe Basic Course (Warburton,
  Kpotufe, Glover, Indiana University 1968, ERIC ED028444, read in the scanned text: "The ordinal
  numerals, with the exception of 'first', are formed by adding /-lia/ to each of the numbers");
  Omniglot and Wiktionary (`evelia`, `etɔ̃lia`, `enelia`, `atɔ̃lia`, `ewolia`).
- **Why not just `suffix="lia"`**: the configured cardinals diverge from the same sources in almost
  every family — units 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 (`deka`/`ɖeka`, `eto`/`etɔ̃`, `atɔ`/`atɔ̃`, `adre`/`adrɛ`,
  `asea`/`asieke`), teens (`ewo kple deka`/`wuiɖeka` …), tens (`blavo eve`/`blaeve`), the tens-units
  connector (`kple`/`vɔ`: `blaeve vɔ ɖeka`), hundreds (`kpeɖe`/`alafa ɖeka`), thousands (`deka
  akpe`/`akpe ɖeka`, noun before multiplier) and zero (`zero`/`nadeke`). No consulted source
  presents the configured forms as a dialect or older spelling. `-lia` would form `etolia`,
  `asealia`, which no source has.
- **Decision (case B)**: a full cardinal rebuild is not an ordinal fix; NTS-20 is opened. Until then
  `<Ordinals>` is removed: `SupportsOrdinals` is `false` and every `ConvertOrdinal` throws
  `NotSupportedException`, `gbãtɔ` included (one value is not a capability). The Ewe cardinals are
  unchanged and their feature now says they are not sourced forms.

## Verification

Test-first: the new Wolof (sourced 1, 2, 3, 8, 10, 12, 24; productive 11, 21, 100, 101, 234, 999,
−2; rejected 1000, 1001, 1110, 2000, 999999, −1000) and Ewe (unsupported; 0, 1, 2, 21 rejected)
scenarios ran red (23 failures, each for the expected reason) before the configuration. New unit
tests: the Wolof guard boundaries, a mechanical check that every Wolof ordinal 2–999 equals its
cardinal + `eel`, and the Ewe withdrawal. A before/after dump of the 73 built-in cultures
(cardinals and ordinals 0–2100, scale samples up to `long.MaxValue`, every variant value, clock
times) differs only on WO and EE ordinal lines: 2327 WO ordinals changed (999 now end in `-eel`,
the values from 1000 throw) and the 2330 EE ordinal lines disappear. No cardinal and no other
culture changed.
