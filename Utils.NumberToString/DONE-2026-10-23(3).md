# NTS-53 — Catalan, Galician and Romanian numeral defects observed by the NTS-52 dump (2026-10-23)

NTS-53 is closed. Starting master: `19663d61` (NTS-52, #658). Sources: `docs/NTS-08-linguistic-sources.md`, section
NTS-53. Opened: NTS-54 (Romanian multiplier agreement). NTS-55 (Catalan/Galician feminine multiplier of *milió* /
*millón*), opened by the first version of the PR, was closed in it at review (below).

Each of the four observations was audited on its own before any change; the causes the ticket suspected were
confirmed, and two more defects were fixed on the user's decision: the Catalan ordinals of the millions, and (at
the review of #659) the feminine multiplier of *milió* / *millón* they exposed.

## Catalan plural *milions* — configuration only

- Cause: `<Scale value="2" string="milió(ns)" />`. The parenthesis appends its content to the singular, so the plural
  was *milió* + *ns* = *milións*; Catalan moves the accent (*milió* / *milions*).
- Source: IEC, *Ortografia catalana* §4.4.2.2a, "dos milions cent mil"; DIEC2 "milió": "Dos milions d'habitants".
- Fix: `mili(ó|ons)`, the alternation already used by the suffix. No textual exception.

## Catalan and Galician 10^9 — `pairedScalesFrom="2"`

- Cause: a static scale 3 named `mil milió(ns)` / `mil mill(ón|óns)`, multiplied like any noun: *un mil milió*, *un
  mil millón*, *dos mil milións cinc-cents milións* (2.5 × 10^9), *un mil milió un milió* (1 001 000 000).
- Sources: DIEC2 "miliard": "Mil milions"; IEC *Revista de Tecnologia* 10 (2022), p. 4-9: "dos mil milions de
  dòlars"; UPC: "els milers de milions s'escriuen […] amb el mot milions"; RAG "eón": "mil millóns de anos".
- Fix: the scale 3 is removed and the scales are paired from the million (`pairedScalesFrom="2"`, the ES/EU primitive
  of NTS-30F): the multiplier of *milió* / *millón* is one complete cardinal of up to six digits.

| Value | CA before | CA after | GL after |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10^9 | un mil milió | mil milions | mil millóns |
| 2 × 10^9 | dos mil milións | dos mil milions | dous mil millóns |
| 2.5 × 10^9 | dos mil milións cinc-cents milións | dos mil cinc-cents milions | dous mil cincocentos millóns |
| 1 001 000 000 | un mil milió un milió | mil un milions | mil un millóns |
| 1 000 001 000 | un mil milió mil | mil milions mil | mil millóns mil |
| 1 000 000 001 | un mil milió un | mil milions un | mil millóns un |
| 10^9 euros | un mil milió d'euros | mil milions d'euros | mil millóns de euros |

The NTS-52 noun connector still sees the paired *milió* as the terminal scale (`GetTerminalScaleIndex` reads a paired
multiplier as one group at its named scale): *mil milions d'euros*, *mil millóns de euros*.

### Large scales

The long scale of the sources (DIEC2 *bilió* "Milió de milions", *trilió* "Milió de bilions"; Ortografia §4.4.3.4 "mil
bilionèsim (de mil bilions)"; RAG *billón* "Un millón de millóns") is exactly a pairing from the million. But neither
language has a generated scale, before or after: no prefix table is configured and `maxNumber="999999999999"`; with
`MaxNumber` lifted, 10^12 throws `InvalidOperationException` (prefix table missing) on master and on the branch alike.
The single `Suffixes` entry stays inert; a future opening must declare one suffix per half of a pair, as FI does,
because `TryGetDynamicScaleInfo` numbers the unnamed upper halves too (ES has the same latent shape).

### Valencian

`ca-ES-valencia` is `baseOn="CA"` and redeclares neither `NumberScale` nor `Ordinals`: it inherits the plural, the
pairing and the ordinals unchanged, and its dump differs from `CA` on exactly the same lines.

### Ordinals

- GL: the guard (1–1999) is unchanged; 10^6 and above still throw `NotSupportedException`.
- CA had no guard above zero: master returned *un milióè*, *dos miliónsè*, *un mil milióè*, which the pairing alone
  would have turned into *dos milionsè*, *mil milionsè*. The user chose to implement a sourced form. The IEC attests
  both *milionè - milionena* and *milionèsim - milionèsima* (GBU §18.5, quadre 18.6) and prefers the *-è/-ena* series
  ("En general, són preferibles els ordinals en -er/-er(a) i en -è/-en(a) […], mentre que els ordinals en
  -èsim/-èsim(a) són d'ús poc freqüent", GIEC §17.2.5), which the Catalan series already uses (*milè*, *centè*).
  The first version of the PR stated that *milionè* does not exist (it is absent from the DIEC2) and produced
  *milionèsim*; the review of #659 corrected the claim, and the user then selected *milionè* (project decision).
  Configuration only: `<OrdinalException value="1000000" string="milionè" />` (the single million drops *un*, like
  *milè*) and `<Ordinal from="milions" to="milionè" />`, with their feminine *milionena*: *milionè*, *dos milionè*, *mil
  milionè*, *dos mil cinc-cents milionè*, the multiples written on the cardinal (GIEC §17.2.5). The other ordinals
  (*milè*, *mil unè*, *un milió unè*) are unchanged.

### Masculine multiplier of *milió* / *millón* (NTS-55, closed here)

- Cause: the feminine caller variant rewrites *dos-cents* → *dues-centes* (CA, `scope="Anywhere"`) and *douscentos* →
  *douscentas* (GL) everywhere, the multiplier of the masculine scale noun included: *dues-centes milions*,
  *douscentas millóns*, and with the new ordinals *dues-centes milionena*.
- Source: GEIEC §12.3.2, "una dos-cents mil milionèsima (part) de segon"; DIEC2 "milió" m.; RAG "millón" substantivo
  masculino.
- Fix: `<ScaleForm scale="2">` whose singular and plural force `gender=masculí` / `gender=masculino` on the multiplier,
  the NTS-30G mechanism of ES; the forced group is shielded from the caller's whole-number passes, so the lower groups
  keep agreeing with the counted noun.

| Value, feminine | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| CA 200 000 000 | dues-centes milions | dos-cents milions |
| CA 200 000 000 000 | dues-centes mil milions | dos-cents mil milions |
| CA 200 200 000 | dues-centes milions dues-centes mil | dos-cents milions dues-centes mil |
| CA ordinal 200 000 000 | (dues-centes milionena with the ordinals alone) | dos-cents milionena |
| CA 200 000 000 pessetes | dues-centes milions de pessetes | dos-cents milions de pessetes |
| GL 200 000 000 | douscentas millóns | douscentos millóns |
| CA 200 000 / GL 200 000 | dues-centes mil / douscentas mil | unchanged |

## Romanian scale connector — generic `<ScaleConnector>`

- Cause: `scaleConnector="de" scaleConnectorThreshold="20"` inserted *de* after every multiplier from 20: *o sută unu de
  mii*, *o sută nouăsprezece de milioane*.
- Rule (GALR 2005, vol. I, pp. 296–297): no *de* after one to nineteen "and the compounds with these numerals in last
  position"; *de* from twenty otherwise. On a scale multiplier m: none if m mod 100 ∈ 1..19, *de* if m ≥ 20.
- No textual replacement: the decision is made on the numeric multiplier.

### Architecture

```xml
<Language scaleConnector="de" scaleConnectorThreshold="20" …>
    <ScaleConnector default="">
        <Substitution numberRange="1..19" numberModulo="100" value="" />
    </ScaleConnector>
</Language>
```

- `ScaleConnectorRules` (immutable: `Default`, `Substitutions` snapshotted in order) and `ScaleConnectorRule`
  (immutable: `Value`, `NumberRange`, `NumberModulo`, `Scales`, `Specificity`), on the same principle as the NTS-52
  `CurrencyNounConnector` without a shared hierarchy: a default; substitutions with one or two constraints,
  `numberRange` (the value of the **whole** multiplier, the `group` of `ConvertRaw`, a paired multiplier included;
  `numberModulo` ≥ 2 is a modifier of the range, not a constraint) and `scales` (the scale index); the most specific
  wins, the first declared at equal specificity, never an ambiguity error. `beforeNoun` was not added (no need).
- Legacy (option B of the ticket, without a break): the `scaleConnector`/`scaleConnectorThreshold` attributes and
  options keep their meaning and act as the **last declared** substitution (`numberRange="threshold.."`): any declared
  substitution takes precedence over them, and they take precedence over the default. The public getters report the
  shorthand only, so `GetConverter("RO").ScaleConnector == "de"` and `ScaleConnectorThreshold == 20`, as on master;
  a converter that configures only the shorthand is byte-identical, its empty connector (`Separator + "" +
  Separator`) included. Unlike NTS-52 the two models are combined rather than rejected together: the combination has
  one defined meaning, and rejecting it would have nulled the Romanian getter.
- Inside a paired multiplier, its own thousands group is decided on its own multiplier (the multiplier is a complete
  cardinal), and the named scale on the whole one (tested: `2500` is seen for 2.5 × 10^9, neither `500` nor `2`).
- XML: `<ScaleConnector>` in the `<Language>` choice (`LanguageType.ScaleConnectorRules`, `ScaleConnectorType`,
  `ScaleConnectorSubstitutionType`); inherited through `baseOn` unless redeclared (replaced as a whole). XSD rejects a
  substitution without `value`, malformed or negative ranges, `scales` below 1, `numberModulo` below 2 and unknown
  attributes; the loader rejects a substitution without constraint, a white-space value or default, a modulo without
  range or leaving no residue in it. `ConnectorRangeChecks` holds the range checks now shared with
  `CurrencyNounConnectorRule` (same behaviour).

| Multiplier | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| 19 000 / 20 000 | nouăsprezece mii / douăzeci de mii | unchanged |
| 101 000, 119 000, 219 000 | o sută unu de mii, o sută nouăsprezece de mii, două sute nouăsprezece de mii | o sută unu mii, o sută nouăsprezece mii, două sute nouăsprezece mii |
| 120 000, 132 000 | o sută douăzeci de mii, o sută treizeci și doi de mii | unchanged |
| 119 × 10^6, 119 × 10^9 | o sută nouăsprezece de milioane / de miliarde | o sută nouăsprezece milioane / miliarde |

Each connector is decided on its own value: *o sută douăzeci de mii de euro* (scale multiplier 120, currency count
120 000), *o sută nouăsprezece mii de euro* (119; 119 000), *o sută nouăsprezece mii nouăsprezece euro* (119; 119 019).

## Romanian currency *un* / *o* — `<Currency><Replacement>`

- Classification: the cardinal *unu* is right (DOOM 3 §4.1.5: *douăzeci și unu*, *o sută unu*, *o mie unu*), and so
  is the compound before a noun; one alone before a noun is attributive (DEX '09 "unu": "Când precedă un substantiv are
  forma un, o"). It is a currency-context defect, not a cardinal one: no global `form` dimension.
- Fix: `<Replacement oldValue="unu" newValue="un" scope="Standalone" />` and `oldValue="una" newValue="o"`, after the
  variants (caller < `forceVariants` < `CurrencyDefinition`) have produced *unu* or *una*: *un euro*, *un leu și un
  ban*, *o liră* (feminine `CurrencyDefinition` or caller `gen=feminin`). Standalone, never `LastWord`: *douăzeci și unu
  de euro*, *o sută unu euro* are untouched. `Convert(1)` stays *unu*, the clock *ora unu* too.

## Multi-group replacements

Not needed and not added. Every observed defect was a structure the existing primitives describe: the upper scale is
part of the multiplier of the lower named one (`pairedScalesFrom`), the connector depends on the numeric multiplier
(`<ScaleConnector>`), the attributive *un*/*o* is local to the currency numeral (`<Currency><Replacement>`). The dump
after the change shows no remaining CA/GL/RO line that these cannot express.
