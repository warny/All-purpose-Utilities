### B. Grouping architecture

The engine split a number into groups of one fixed width (the highest digit level) and rendered each group
in isolation. Two things could not be represented:

- **Mixed widths.** The Indian system names 10^3, 10^5, 10^7 …: the units group has three digits, the next
  ones two. With uniform groups of three, 10^5 falls inside the thousands group, so no scale name can ever be
  reached there (`UniformGroups_CannotNameTheLakh` shows *one hundred thousand* for 100 000 and *one lakh* for
  1 000 000 whatever the names). New generic primitive **`groupPattern`** (XML attribute,
  `NumberToStringConverterOptions.GroupPattern`, `NumberToStringConverter.GroupPattern`): the widths of the
  successive groups from the units upward, the last repeating (`3,2`). Default: the historical uniform width.
  Snapshotted, validated (1 ≤ width ≤ highest digit level; XSD pattern; parser), inherited through `baseOn`,
  copied by the options clone, used by the `maxNumber` naming check, rejected with `<OrdinalScale>` (which
  locates a scale at 10^(index × width)). BigInteger and negative values follow the same path.
- **Context of a position.** A group rendered after a higher non-zero group reads its leading zero positions
  in Vietnamese (*không trăm linh năm*) and marks them with 零 in Chinese; inside a group, Chinese marks a
  skipped position (一千零五) and Vietnamese joins a lone unit with *linh*. New generic primitive
  **`innerString` / `innerBuildString`** on `<Digit>` (`DigitType.InnerString`/`InnerBuildString`): used instead
  of `string` / `buildString` when a higher digit of the whole number (same group or higher group) is non-zero.
  The most significant group keeps `buildString` for its leading zeros. Copied with the digits (exposed
  `Groups`, scoped tables, options clone); `<Fusion>` edges are validated in both contexts.
- **East Asian 10^4 grouping needed no engine change**: `groupSize="4"` with a fourth digit level and static
  scale names (万/億/兆, 만/억/조, 万/亿); only the spacing (empty separator for JA/ZH, a `groupWithScale`
  trigger attaching 만/억/조 for KO) and the zero/一十 handling (inner forms + replacements for ZH) were
  configuration.

Neither primitive names a language: the converter has no language-specific branch, the words stay in XML.

### E. Ordinal interaction

Every NTS-08 guard is unchanged (verified on the dump: no ordinal moved between a value and
`NotSupportedException`): RU ≥ 2000, PL ≥ 2000, VN ≥ 1000, HI ≥ 100 000, JA/KO > 1000, ZH > 100 except round
hundreds, EU 0 and > 1000, ES compound limits, SV above a million. Ordinals changed inside a supported domain,
only through the spelling of the cardinal:

- EU 600–699: `seirehungarren` → `seiehungarren`, `seirehun eta …garren` → `seiehun eta …garren` (100 values;
  rule 7 writes *seiehun*).
- HI: every ordinal containing 5 (`पांचवाँ` → `पाँचवाँ`, `पांच सौवाँ` → `पाँच सौवाँ`; 660 lines of the dump
  across the gender variants) and the clock (`साढ़े पांच` → `साढ़े पाँच`, `पौने पांच` → `पौने पाँच`, 72 lines).
  Each changed line equals the old one with `पांच` replaced by `पाँच`.

The cardinal no longer blocks the NTS-28 items for RU/PL from 2000, VN from 1000, HI from 100 000, JA/KO above
1000, ZH above 100 and EU above 1000; those ordinals still need their own source.
