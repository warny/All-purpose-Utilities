## Rule adopted

A cardinal from 101 whose last group ends in a bare *jeden* (`number % 100 == 1`) is a **higher compound numeral
ending in *jeden***, indeclinable like *dvadsaťjeden* (S1, 226; S2). In a fraction with a fraction noun, the numeral
keeps its absolute form and the noun alone takes the case: the plural of the requested oblique case, the genitive
plural in the direct cases (S3; the rule NTS-40 already applies to 21–91 and 121). The class is grammatical, not a
numeric ending test: it is the same `rest % 10 == 1 && rest != 11` predicate that already defined the *-jeden*
compounds, now without the carve-out for `rest == 1`; 1 (declined *jedna*) is handled before it, 11, 111, 1011
(*jedenásť*, declined like *päť*) stay outside it.

The two questions of the ticket are separate and both answered by S1: (1) the bare *jeden* is indeclinable because
the numeral belongs to the higher compounds ending in *jeden*; (2) the scale noun before it is not the last component
and stays in the absolute form, which S1's table shows for *milión* and which the plugin already does for 1 000 021.
No other group changes: the reading is the nominative cardinal with the noun in the case. The remaining weakness,
stated as such: no source shows a bare *jeden* right after a scale noun in an oblique case; the reading follows from
the class rule, as 101 does.

### Spelling

The converter's separated groups are kept (*sto jeden*, *tisíc jeden*, *dvetisíc jeden*): S1 admits *stojeden / sto
jeden* (211–213, 226–227) and the NTS-30B spelling of the SK configuration is not re-opened here.

## Cause of the previous closure

`SlovakOrdinalLanguageSpecifics.DeclineLastComponent` classified `number % 100 == 1` as `LastComponent.BareOne`
before the *-jeden* test, and `TryBuildObliqueNounFraction` returned `false` for `BareOne` outside the accusative
("no sourced case form", NTS-40). The slash denominator already treated `BareOne` like `Indeclinable` (*lomené číslom
sto jeden*).
