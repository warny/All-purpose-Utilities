# Utils.NumberToString audit closures — 2026-10-07 (2)

NTS-19 (Wolof cardinals: orthography and thousands) is closed. The Wolof cardinals 0–999 999 and the
ordinals are now built on a single convention: the spelling of decree 2005-992 and the structure of
the grammars. The ordinal guard from 1000 is lifted; only zero and the round thousands stay rejected,
as deliberate limitations. The million is not audited and stays outside the domain (opened NTS-21).
Sources and decisions are recorded in `docs/NTS-08-linguistic-sources.md` ("NTS-19 — Wolof
cardinals").

Configuration and tests only, plus the guard's narrowed rule: `NumberToStringConverter*.cs`, the
XSD, `NumberScale`, `<OrdinalScale>` and `<OrdinalComposition>` are untouched. The thousands use the
existing `groupConnector`/`groupConnectorThreshold` and eight `onScale="1"` replacements.

## Sources

- **Decree 2005-992 of 21 October 2005** (orthography and word separation of Wolof), full text
  (labo-styloculture.com copy of the *Journal officiel*, article 4802; jo.gouv.sn was unreachable):
  Art. 10 (one accent on a closed long vowel), Art. 12 (a determiner is a separate word), Art. 13
  ("la marque -u/-i du rapport complétant/complété est rattachée au terme complété": `néegu ñax`,
  `ay saami kaani`, `fukkéelu garab gi` "le dixième arbre"), Art. 20 (a suffix keeps an invariable
  spelling despite vowel harmony), Art. 21 (hyphen between the elements of a compound word:
  `gaynde-géej`, `mbaam-àll`, reduplications), Art. 23 (`ñaari tomb` "deux points").
- Kosogorova 2023 and Robert 2021 (read for NTS-16); P. A. Gaye, *Practical Course in Wolof*, Peace
  Corps 1980 (ERIC ED226616, numbers and money, pp. 66–67); Fal 1999 via S. Voisin (CNRS DDL),
  "Possession adnominale dans différentes variétés de wolof", *Afrikanistik-Aegyptologie-Online* 2021
  (`ñaari potu meew`); Boston University, The 200 Word Project, Wolof numbers; Wiktionary's Wolof
  number list; Janga Wolof; Omniglot.

## Decisions

- **Spaces, not hyphens** (`juróom benn`, `ñaar fukk`, `juróom benn fukk`). Every grammar writes
  spaces (Kosogorova, Robert, Gaye, BU, Wiktionary); the decree hyphenates the elements of a
  *compound word* (lexicalized: `gaynde-géej` "requin"), and a numeral phrase is not one (Art. 12
  separates a determiner from its noun). Only learner material (Janga Wolof) hyphenates.
- **The connective `-i`** is the genitival connective between a numeral and what it counts (Gaye: "a
  linker and indicates a relationship between the number and the object counted"; Robert: "the
  number of hundreds or thousands are compound forms with the genitival linker"; Ngom: plural
  marker). `téeméer` and `junni` are counted nouns, so their multiplier takes it, attached as the
  decree requires (`ñaari tomb`; Fal `ñaari potu meew`), on the multiplier's last element (Gaye
  `juróom benn-i dërëm`): `ñaari téeméer`, `juróom benni téeméer`, `ñaari junni`, `fukki junni`,
  `téeméeri junni` (BU `fukki téeméeri junni`). The tens are not a counted noun in that sense:
  `ñaar fukk` in every source. Kosogorova's `ñaar tééméér` / `ñaar juuni` are the variant without
  `-i`, which Gaye says is optional with `ñaar` only: accepted, not produced.
- **`junni` alone for 1000** (Robert, BU, Wiktionary, Janga; Kosogorova `juuni`): `onValue="1"`
  replacement. `junni` over `juuni` (Kosogorova only) and `junne` (Omniglot only).
- **`ak` before the part below the thousands**: `groupConnector="ak"` with
  `groupConnectorThreshold="1000"` (inserted only when the lower group is non-zero). Kosogorova
  `juuni ak juróom ñaar` (1007), `ñaar juuni ak juróom ñett fukk` (2080); Robert `ñaar-i junni ak
  juróóm ñett fukk`; Janga `junni ak benn`.
- **Zero `tus`** (BU "Tus (Zero)", Wiktionary, Janga `tus / dara`, Wikipedia `tus`). `dara` is the
  indefinite "anything/nothing", `sero` a French loan listed by no source. Two independent sources
  list `tus` as the number, so `Convert(0)` = `tus`; the ordinal of zero is still rejected.
- **Ordinal spelling `-éel`** (revises NTS-16's `-eel`). The decree prints `fukkéelu garab gi` and keeps
  suffix spellings invariable (Art. 20); NTS-16 had only a summary of the decree and inferred `-eel`
  from the academic transcriptions, which are phonetic (Kosogorova, Robert) and, for Robert, use
  double accents the decree does not.
- **Ordinals from 1000** apply the general rule (suffix on the last element): `junni ak bennéel`,
  `ñaari junni ak bennéel` — productive, not individually attested.
- **Round thousands rejected** (`WolofOrdinalLanguageSpecifics`): their last element is the vowel-final
  `junni` and the two examples disagree (Omniglot `junneel`, Janga Wolof `junniéél`). A deliberate
  limitation, not open work, unless a grammar or dictionary settles the junction.
- **Kept**: `ñett fukk` for 30 (Kosogorova; `fanweer` "days of a month", given by Robert and Gaye as an irregular primary number, is an accepted variant, not produced), `ñent` (Robert, Kosogorova, Gaye; `ñeent` in BU and Wiktionary is an accepted variant,
  not produced), `juróom`, `téeméer` (decree accent rule), `pojint`, `ci`, `minus` (out of scope).
- **Million**: not audited (BU gives `fukki téeméeri junni` or `benn milyoŋ`, the configuration has an
  unused `milyon`); `maxNumber="999999"` kept, NTS-21 opened.

## Verification

Test-first: the rewritten Wolof feature (cardinals 0–999 999 including 1000, 1001, 1005, 1007, 1010,
1011, 1100, 1111, 1599, 2000, 2001, 2080, 2100, 2345, 3000–6000, 10000, 11000, 20000, 21000, 99000,
100000, 200000, 999999, −1000, −1001; ordinals; rejections) and the unit tests ran red (69 failures, the rejections already holding)
before the configuration. New mechanical sweeps over 1–999 999: ordinal = cardinal + `éel` except 1
and the rejected round thousands; `ak` after `junni` exactly when a lower group exists, never doubled
or trailing, no hyphen, the connective on every multiplier of junni from 2000. A before/after dump
of the 73 built-in cultures differs only on WO lines (2269 cardinals, 2236 ordinals); no other
culture changed. Ordinals above 999 999 now throw the engine's out-of-range
`InvalidOperationException` like the other languages, instead of the guard's `NotSupportedException`.
